## 2. Is this one lion or two?

There are two distinct photo sequences with noticeably different lighting, posture and mane appearance. That does **not** prove two individuals: direct sun versus deep shade can substantially change how coat and mane colour look, and the sequences could have been taken at different positions or times.

Until the photographs are matched against keeper-verified individual reference images, this record should say:

- at least **one mane-bearing lion** is documented;
- the six images may show the same individual or more than one individual;
- exact identity is unresolved.

Taronga's current Lion Pride Lands page names three males connected with the current Dubbo collection: adult male **Lwazi** in the main pride and brothers **Bahati** and **Jabari**, who form a bachelor coalition. That makes several identities plausible, not one identity proven.

## 6. Lion social life — flexible, not a cartoon hierarchy

Lions are unusually social cats. Prides commonly centre on related females and their offspring, while males may live singly or form coalitions. Young males often disperse from their natal pride as they approach maturity. Taronga specifically uses this natural-history context when explaining the separation of Bahati and Jabari into a bachelor coalition.

Useful field observations include:
- distance between individuals;
- resting proximity;
- grooming or rubbing;
- following;
- play;
- feeding access;
- scent-marking;
- vocalising;
- movement between shade, open ground and elevated features.

Avoid translating a single pose into labels such as "alpha", "dominant", "angry", "sad", "lazy" or "stressed" without repeated behavioural evidence and context.

## 7. Resting and climbing — what the photos can and cannot tell us

Lions can spend large portions of the day resting, especially in warm conditions, but a still photograph cannot establish sleep duration, heat stress, illness or daily routine. In Josh's shaded sequence, the defensible description is simply **lying/resting posture in shade**.

The elevated-log sequence is also valuable: the lion is visibly standing on a large timber feature. Elevated structures can provide vantage, choice and environmental complexity in managed habitats, but this one sequence does not reveal why the lion climbed it or how frequently it uses it.

A good field-journal rule is: **record the action and substrate first; interpret function only when evidence supports it.**

## 8. Wild conservation — current context

The 2024 IUCN assessment used in Dubbozoo's audit lists *Panthera leo* as **Vulnerable**. The IUCN Cat Specialist Group describes major pressures including habitat and habitat-quality loss, prey depletion, poaching/bushmeat pressure and conflict with people.

The Cat Specialist Group's current synthesis reports that:
- lion range is highly fragmented;
- some populations are stable or recovering while others continue to decline;
- in 2025, extant range was estimated at about **566,675 km²**, around **6.13% of historical range**;
- it cites a 2025 estimate of roughly **22,000–25,000 adult and subadult lions in Africa**.

That estimate is not interchangeable with IUCN "mature individuals", is not a zoo count and should not be described as an exact global census.

## 9. Taxonomy matters

Modern lion taxonomy commonly recognises two subspecies:
- *Panthera leo leo* — India plus West/Central African lineages;
- *Panthera leo melanochaita* — Eastern and Southern African lineages.

"**African lion**" is therefore a useful zoo/common label but is not enough to identify a photographed lion's exact managed ancestry. Dubbozoo should not assign one of the two subspecies to Josh's lion without a studbook, pedigree or Taronga statement about that individual.

## 10. Historical Dubbo 'Asiatic lion' signage — lineage unresolved

A 6 May 1995 Dubbo Zoo visitor diary explicitly describes an **'Asiatic Lions'** paddock at Western Plains Zoo. A 2008 discussion with former staff/long-time observers contains inconsistent recollections about whether those historical animals were Asian-African hybrids or African lineage.

No authenticated Dubbo studbook establishing pure Asiatic ancestry has been retrieved. Historical sign wording is evidence of how an exhibit was labelled, **not genetic evidence**. Do not create a second biological species entry from that historical label alone.
