# African lion — Dubbo Zoo researched guide

**Scientific name:** *Panthera leo* · **IUCN:** Vulnerable (VU) at species level · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026. **Scope:** species background and confirmed zoo attraction, not a photographic observation.

## Ecology and social structure
Lions are among the most social cats. A pride often includes related females and their offspring, while a male or coalition may hold tenure for a period. Pride membership, sizes and dominance relationships are **variable**, not fixed rules: lone lions, dispersing males and shifting coalitions are normal. Lionesses commonly collaborate in hunting, while males also hunt and scavenge. Species prey range from medium antelopes to larger hoofed mammals, and differs with local ecosystems. Lions inhabit grasslands, wooded savannah and mixed brush where they can stalk.

Lions rely on scent-marking, calls and body posture to communicate. Males' conspicuous manes can signal age/condition and reduce vulnerability in some encounters; the appearance and colour of a mane are not reliable ways to give an individual a precise age. Taronga notes that a roar may carry for kilometres, depending on conditions.

## Wild conservation
Lions are **Vulnerable**, according to [Taronga's lion profile](https://www.taronga.org.au/wildlife-and-conservation/animals/detail/african-lion). Habitat conversion, fragmentation, conflict with pastoralists, prey decline, disease and poorly managed retaliatory killing threaten many populations. Their distribution has contracted severely compared with their historical African range; the species also survives as an isolated population in India. The broad common name 'African lion' does not mean that all *Panthera leo* live in Africa.

A reliable global number requires assessment year, range and whether the figure means mature breeding animals or all ages. This guide deliberately **does not invent an October 2026 wild count** or extrapolate from regional parks.

## Verified Dubbo visitor information
The [Taronga African Adventure itinerary](https://www.taronga.org.au/dubbo-zoo/plan/get-the-most-out-of-your-visit/african-adventure) includes **Lion Pride Lands** at **map 17S** with a guided patrol activity. Taronga's [African lion species page](https://www.taronga.org.au/wildlife-and-conservation/animals/detail/african-lion) also states that Dubbo's lions can be seen at Lion Pride Lands. This does not prove which individual animals were on public exhibit on a particular day or that a paid patrol is available on a given date.

Keeping and breeding endangered or vulnerable zoo species requires animal-welfare and population-management planning. A managed lion pride in an exhibit is not a proxy for an intact wild pride ecosystem.

## Field-guide prompts
- Compare the posture and faces of individuals, while avoiding unverified sex, age or family claims.
- Do you see scent-marking, grooming, resting or moving together? Describe **what is visible** rather than assuming motives.
- Can you locate areas where an animal can withdraw from crowds?
- Why might the presence of lions indicate a need for adequate wild prey and large connected landscapes?
