# Asian elephant — Dubbo Zoo deep research guide

**Scientific name:** *Elephas maximus* · **IUCN:** Endangered (EN) · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026. No user photo sighting documented.

## Anatomy, family and ecology
Asian elephants are large herbivores and **ecosystem engineers**: their movement, browsing, tree disturbance and seed dispersal modify vegetation and shape opportunities for other species. They use a sensitive, muscular trunk for smell, communication, drinking (transferring water into the mouth), manipulation and exploration. Unlike African elephants they have relatively smaller ears; Asian females usually lack large external tusks, while some males also have very small or no visible tusks. A visible tusk is **not sufficient by itself** to assign a sex or species in a distant photo.

Female relatives often form long-lived social units with calves; mature males may travel alone or loosely associate in groups. Elephants communicate with sound, touch, scents and lower-frequency rumbles. They need broad connected habitats, plentiful food/water and opportunities to move; farming expansion can create dangerous clashes.

## Conservation
The IUCN lists Asian elephants as **Endangered**; habitat loss/fragmentation, infrastructure, illegal killing/trafficking and conflict with people are central pressures. Taronga's [2026 public statement](https://www.taronga.org.au/about/newsroom/media-releases/asian-elephant-calf-statement) references **about 40,000** in the wild as an estimate, not an exact count or a measure of mature breeding females. Elephant conservation requires protecting corridors and livelihoods as well as combating poaching.

## Dubbo: recent, sensitive and dated record
Taronga lists Asian elephants in **Wild Asia** and a **12:00 pm keeper talk** on its [species page](https://www.taronga.org.au/wildlife-and-conservation/animals/detail/asian-elephant). In a **15 January 2026** report, Taronga confirmed a **stillborn calf** to elephant **Anjalee** following a difficult labour, and reported her receiving close support from **Thong Dee, Porntip and Kanlaya**. These were the names and circumstances documented *at that time*. The statement noted the female group was doing well, but the zoo's January report is not evidence of the status of individuals in October 2026. Be respectful of the animal-welfare and staff implications; do not turn a stillbirth into a spectacle.

A historical Taronga story about calf **Sabai** provides keeper observations of bathing/skin care and family bonds, but it is not a present-day schedule or proof Sabai is visible now.

### Deep scientific evidence and conservation context
- **IUCN EN A2c:** the 2020 IUCN assessment, last assessed **18 September 2019**, derives its Endangered conclusion from an **inferred decline of at least 50% across three generations**, supported by changes in occupancy and habitat quality. This is not a claim that exactly half of counted individuals died in the past year. [IUCN assessment](https://doi.org/10.2305/IUCN.UK.2020-3.RLTS.T7140A45818198.en).
- **Wild numbers need a year and metric:** Taronga's 2026 public statement cites **~40,000** as an estimate. Historical country-by-country figures such as the IUCN 2008 assessment's **41,410–52,345** are not a current verified census and should not be conflated with mature breeders. [Taronga 2026](https://www.taronga.org.au/about/newsroom/media-releases/asian-elephant-calf-statement), [IUCN 2020](https://doi.org/10.2305/IUCN.UK.2020-3.RLTS.T7140A45818198.en).
- **Sexual differences are nuanced:** Asian females seldom develop externally large tusks; males may also be tuskless. Relative body size and tusks alone cannot confirm the sexes of these two photographs.
- **Ecological engineers:** forest elephants consume and move seeds, change vegetation and create pathways; these ecological roles depend on habitat connectivity and adequate forest space. [Taronga Asian elephant account](https://www.taronga.org.au/wildlife-and-conservation/animals/detail/asian-elephant).
- **Wild threat mix:** forest clearing for farms, roads and plantations can fragment routes, while crop raiding/human-elephant conflict, poaching and illegal trade are major hazards. Community-safe landscape connections, non-lethal conflict prevention and enforcement matter more than simplistic claims that a zoo animal's birth directly recovers a wild population. [IUCN 2020](https://doi.org/10.2305/IUCN.UK.2020-3.RLTS.T7140A45818198.en).
- **Zoo history:** [Taronga's Gung transfer account](https://www.taronga.org.au/about/newsroom/posts/gung-settles-in-at-dubbo) confirms his **2018** move and keeper-managed social introductions. The posted keeper talk time on that older page was **11:45 am** while [Taronga's current species page](https://www.taronga.org.au/wildlife-and-conservation/animals/detail/asian-elephant) lists **12:00 pm** — follow today's visitor schedule rather than assuming the old time.

### Field questions for young visitors
1. How do the ears and trunk differ from an African elephant's?
2. How can a trunk help an elephant grasp, smell, communicate and transfer water?
3. Can you see two elephants clearly? Do you have enough evidence to name either?
4. What are the fence, pool/wallow and tall poles for? If uncertain, ask a keeper.
5. Why do elephants need movement corridors in their wild habitats?
