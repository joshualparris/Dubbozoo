# Asian elephant — Dubbo Zoo deep research guide

**Scientific name:** *Elephas maximus* · **IUCN:** Endangered (EN) · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026. No user photo sighting documented.

## Anatomy, family and ecology
Asian elephants are large herbivores and **ecosystem engineers**: their movement, browsing, tree disturbance and seed dispersal modify vegetation and shape opportunities for other species. They use a sensitive, muscular trunk for smell, communication, drinking (transferring water into the mouth), manipulation and exploration. Unlike African elephants they have relatively smaller ears; Asian females usually lack large external tusks, while some males also have very small or no visible tusks. A visible tusk is **not sufficient by itself** to assign a sex or species in a distant photo.

Female relatives often form long-lived social units with calves; mature males may travel alone or loosely associate in groups. Elephants communicate with sound, touch, scents and lower-frequency rumbles. They need broad connected habitats, plentiful food/water and opportunities to move; farming expansion can create dangerous clashes.

## Conservation
The IUCN lists Asian elephants as **Endangered**; habitat loss/fragmentation, infrastructure, illegal killing/trafficking and conflict with people are central pressures. Taronga's [2026 public statement](https://www.taronga.org.au/about/newsroom/media-releases/asian-elephant-calf-statement) references **about 40,000** in the wild as an estimate, not an exact count or a measure of mature breeding females. Elephant conservation requires protecting corridors and livelihoods as well as combating poaching.

## Dubbo: recent, sensitive and dated record
Taronga lists Asian elephants in **Wild Asia** and a **12:00 pm keeper talk** on its [species page](https://www.taronga.org.au/wildlife-and-conservation/animals/detail/asian-elephant). In a **15 January 2026** report, Taronga confirmed a **stillborn calf** to elephant **Anjalee** following a difficult labour, and reported her receiving close support from **Thong Dee, Porntip and Kanlaya**. These were the names and circumstances documented *at that time*. The statement noted the female group was doing well, but the zoo's January report is not evidence of the status of individuals in October 2026. Be respectful of the animal-welfare and staff implications; do not turn a stillbirth into a spectacle.

A historical Taronga story about calf **Sabai** provides keeper observations of bathing/skin care and family bonds, but it is not a present-day schedule or proof Sabai is visible now.
