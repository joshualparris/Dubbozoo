# Common hippopotamus — Dubbo Zoo researched guide

**Species:** *Hippopotamus amphibius* · **IUCN:** Vulnerable (VU) (check assessment version when refreshing) · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026. No personal photographs verified.

## Not the same animal as a pygmy hippo
Dubbo exhibits the **common hippopotamus**; Taronga's **pygmy hippos (*Choeropsis liberiensis*) are at Taronga Zoo Sydney**, according to the zoo's 2025 species reporting. Mixing their conservation statuses or natural histories would be a serious field-guide error.

Common hippos are enormously heavy, semi-aquatic mammals with dense skin, nostrils, ears and eyes positioned high on the head. They rest in or near rivers/lakes by day to conserve moisture and commonly move onto land to **graze** at night. They are **not fish-eating carnivores** in ordinary feeding ecology. Hippos can move quickly in shallow water by pushing or walking along the bottom, and are powerful, potentially dangerous wild animals.

Group relationships frequently centre on available water, with territorial males defending stretches and groups of females/young sharing resting areas. One still photo cannot reveal dominance, maternal care, conflict or stress without context.

## Conservation
The IUCN categorises common hippos as **Vulnerable**; main pressures include habitat change, human–wildlife conflict, unregulated hunting for meat/ivory and unstable water conditions. Human safety, equitable river access and wetland habitat restoration all matter. See [peer-reviewed 2024 habitat/population study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e40186) as one regional illustration; its Ethiopia measurements **must not** be treated as a global count.

## Child-friendly observation prompts
1. Can you find the position of the nostrils, ears and eyes?
2. Is an animal resting, grazing or moving through the pool?
3. What fresh-water resources must be conserved to support wild hippos and other species?
4. How can hippo behaviour that seems slow or gentle still require strict distance and barriers?
