# Plains zebra — Dubbo Zoo deep research and taxonomy note

**Scientific name:** *Equus quagga* is the commonly accepted species binomial; Taronga's contemporary page displays older usage *Equus burchellii*. **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026. No zebra photographs submitted.

## Ecology: a grazing equid
Plains zebras are wild African equids, relatives of horses and donkeys, adapted to grasslands, savannahs and open woodlands. Stripe patterns differ among individuals and can aid research-based photo identification **when reference photos exist**. Zebras graze on grass and travel to suitable forage and water, commonly mixing in landscapes with other grazers. Social units include breeding groups and bachelor groups, but they are not static. A group is not proof that every animal is related.

The purpose of stripes is actively studied. Competing ideas include biting-fly deterrence, motion effects and thermoregulation. Do **not** present Taronga's old simplified 'lion confusion' story as definitively settled evolutionary science.

## Taxonomy and a live source discrepancy
Taronga's [current zebra profile](https://www.taronga.org.au/wildlife-and-conservation/animals/detail/plains-zebra) gives **'Equus burchellii'** and **IUCN 'Least Concern'**, while its [2024 Dubbo zoo foal announcement](https://www.taronga.org.au/about/newsroom/media-releases/dubbo-zoo-welcomes-zebra-foal) uses **Near Threatened** for its plains zebra, and even calls the other recognised zebra species 'subspecies'. These sources mix taxonomy, dates and assessments. **Do not silently choose one IUCN status or assert a new Red List change without confirming the current taxon-matched IUCN assessment and year.** The status is stored as **needs authoritative recheck**, not made-up certainty.

Common zebra categories are **plains, mountain and Grevy's zebras**, each with distinct scientific classifications. Older references to Burchell's zebra often describe a particular plains-zebra form/subspecies, not an interchangeable name for every zebra species.

## Taronga and a dated conservation example
Taronga's [African Savannah precinct](https://www.taronga.org.au/dubbo-zoo/precincts-and-pitstops/african-savannah) explicitly lists zebra alongside giraffe, white rhino and eland. The [Giants & Aussie Icons visitor itinerary](https://www.taronga.org.au/dubbo-zoo/plan/get-the-most-out-of-your-visit/giants-aussie-icons) puts a **giraffe-and-zebra stop at 14F**. These are visitor-area references, not a census.

Taronga's 2024 zebra foal story describes pressures such as livestock competition, drought and hunting and its **Beads for Wildlife / Northern Rangelands Trust** community partnership in northern Kenya. This illustrates one approach to making coexistence economically viable but should **not** be converted into an unverified claim that a specific sales volume increased zebra population.
