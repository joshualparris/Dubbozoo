## 2. Why a siamang is an ape, not a monkey

Siamangs are the **largest living gibbons** and, like other apes, have **no tail**. Their adult head/body length is approximately **75–90 cm**, and mass can reach roughly **14 kg**, varying by individual and source. Their shoulders and extended arms are specialised for **brachiation**, swinging hand-over-hand between supports, while their feet can grip branches and their arms may be raised for balance when walking upright.

Two distinguishing adaptations are: (1) an inflatable **gular sac**, a grey or pinkish throat pouch amplifying resonant vocalisations, and (2) **partial webbing between the second and third toes**, reflected by the name *syndactylus*. Neither detail can reliably be diagnosed from these distant frames.

Sources: [Smithsonian National Zoo](https://nationalzoo.si.edu/animals/siamang), [San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance](https://animals.sandiegozoo.org/animals/siamang), [Taronga species profile](https://www.taronga.org.au/wildlife-and-conservation/animals/detail/siamang).

## 3. What those extraordinary calls mean

Siamangs make some of the loudest calls **among gibbons**. Their inflatable throat pouch helps produce deep *booms* as well as higher *wows* and barks. Bonded pairs may sing coordinated **duets** used in territorial communication and social bonding. Singing can accompany energetic displays, but a still photo of an animal hanging from a rope is **not evidence of singing**.

In **March 2026**, Taronga keeper Grace Humphery said **Kasarna and Kasem** were still developing their distinctive duet and that their calls could be heard up to **three kilometres** away. That is a *reported keeper estimate*, not a measurement from these photos. Mornings are often good for listening, though calling is not guaranteed.

Sources: [Taronga, 30 March 2026](https://www.taronga.org.au/about/newsroom/posts/call-of-the-siamangs-echoes-this-easter), [Smithsonian's gibbon overview](https://nationalzoo.si.edu/animals/news/get-know-our-gibbons).

## 4. Native range, food, social groups and young

**Range:** the forests of **Sumatra, Indonesia**, and the **Malay Peninsula**, including Peninsular Malaysia and limited parts of southern Thailand. Siamangs inhabit tropical and montane forests and spend much of their time above ground. They feed primarily on **fruit and young leaves**, with some flowers and occasional animal matter. Compared with some other gibbons, leaves comprise a substantial proportion of their diet.

They commonly live in small family groups containing a bonded pair and dependent young. Young animals may disperse after maturity. Pairs often duet, but a species-level account of social behaviour does **not** prove a particular zoo animal's relationships from photographs. Gestation is broadly **seven to eight months**, and generally one infant is born. Taronga suggests typical lifespans around **30 years in the wild** and up to **40 years in zoos**; these are not ages of any photographed ape.

Sources: [Smithsonian](https://nationalzoo.si.edu/animals/siamang), [San Diego Zoo](https://animals.sandiegozoo.org/animals/siamang), [University of Wisconsin's Primate Info Net](https://primate.wisc.edu/primate-info-net/pin-factsheets/pin-factsheet-siamang/), [Taronga](https://www.taronga.org.au/wildlife-and-conservation/animals/detail/siamang).

## 7. Endangered in the wild: what the evidence supports

The species is **Endangered (EN)**; the IUCN assessment commonly cited is from **2020**, with a **declining** population trend. [Mammal Diversity Database](https://www.mammaldiversity.org/taxon/1000743/) confirms the status, range and scientific classification. Sources consulted do **not** establish a reliable current global count, so no precise wild total is published here.

Chief threats include **forest loss and fragmentation** from agriculture (including expanding oil palm cultivation), roads and resource extraction, and **illegal hunting/capture for the pet trade**. Small forest remnants can be disconnected from other siamang groups.

**Actions with an evidence caveat:**
- Support protection and connection of *existing* wild forests and legitimate in-situ conservation.
- Taronga encourages **certified sustainable palm oil**. It can improve practice but **is not a blanket guarantee of no historic deforestation**: a 2018 satellite study found lower deforestation associated with certification while noting selection effects and limited forest remaining on certified concessions. [Taronga campaign](https://www.taronga.org.au/about/newsroom/posts/siamang-duo-debuts-at-dubbo-zoo) · [Carlson et al., PNAS 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5776786/).
- Taronga's [They're Calling On You initiative](https://www.taronga.org.au/about/newsroom/posts/theyre-calling-on-you) supports mobile-device reuse/recycling and conservation. **Do not equate one recycled phone to a quantified amount of siamang habitat saved.**
- Never support the illegal primate-pet trade. [San Diego Zoo](https://animals.sandiegozoo.org/animals/siamang).

## 8. Things to look for next time (family field guide)

1. **Ropes:** can you see a siamang grip a rope with one hand? Does it use both arms in sequence?
2. **No tail:** watch how it balances using its limbs.
3. **Throat sac:** if it sings, does the pouch inflate? Do **not** confuse the pouch with an injury.
4. **Duet:** can you distinguish two sets of calls? Record a sound only if allowed; photos cannot prove it.
5. **Rainforest:** what would happen if roads and farms separated forest trees into isolated patches?
