# Addax — Dubbozoo researched profile

**Checklist ID:** 1 / 61 · **Taxon:** *Addax nasomaculatus* · **Conservation assessment:** Critically Endangered.  
**Researched:** 18 October 2026 · **Evidence class:** published Taronga records and taxon background; **no photo from Josh verified for this entry**.

## Species identity and identification

A pale antelope with long, conspicuously twisted horns in both sexes, broad hooves and seasonal coat changes. Calves often stay concealed in vegetation early in life rather than following the herd constantly.

**Native range / habitat:** Sahara/Sahel desert and semi-desert habitats. Where the exhibit label lacks a confirmed scientific identification, this description is **conditional** on future species verification.

## Feeding, behaviour and natural history

An arid-land browser/grazer feeding on grasses, herbs and shrub material when available. It conserves moisture exceptionally well, obtains water from food and moves widely where rainfall produces temporary forage; it should not be described as needing no water whatsoever.

**Field question:** What do its screw-like horns and wide feet reveal about living in desert terrain? Look for an observable feature or action rather than assuming an unseen purpose, personality, sex, health or family relationship from a single image.

## Wild conservation and scientific interpretation

Uncontrolled hunting, military disturbance, oil exploration/infrastructure and livestock competition contributed to catastrophic collapse of wild herds; protected reintroductions and well-managed zoo breeding must be evaluated separately.

**Status caution:** Critically Endangered. Assessment authority, taxonomic rank, year and whether quoted quantities count all individuals or only mature breeders should be matched before adding headline population figures. Historical counts and today's population should never be substituted for one another.

## Specific Dubbo zoo evidence and history

Taronga reported two calves in late 2022; its later addax article documented a 14-animal group comprising 11 on exhibit and three bachelor males behind the scenes at the **time of reporting**, not a verified October 2026 count. Some Taronga articles disagree dramatically on surviving wild totals; do not portray dated estimates as a fresh census.

**This is not a current 2026 zoo census.** A public map establishes an exhibit label, while a dated Taronga news release supports only the event stated for that date. Animals may be transferred, housed behind the scenes or absent from public view; no names, sexes, photo dates or exact current herd sizes are invented here.

## Zoo-field observation and evidence protocol

Observe from designated public areas, noting the actual date/time, site map grid, number visible and visible feeding/resting/moving/social interactions. Record habitat features, weather and vantage point when known. Do not diagnose health, captivity stress or breeding success from one still photo. Conservation breeding supports genetic management but does not replace protection of wild habitat or prove reintroduced survival.
