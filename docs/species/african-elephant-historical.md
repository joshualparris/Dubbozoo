## Species identity and identification

African elephants have very large ears and a muscular trunk; the two modern recognised species differ in size, tusks, ear outline, range and genetics. A historical exhibit label simply saying 'African elephant' is not enough to establish the specific lineage of every animal kept at Dubbo.

**Native range / habitat:** African savannah/forest depending on species; living African elephants are classified as African savannah and forest elephants in modern IUCN taxonomy. Where the exhibit label lacks a confirmed scientific identification, this description is **conditional** on future species verification.

## Feeding, behaviour and natural history

Elephants are herbivorous landscape engineers, moving seeds, opening vegetation and using social learning. Females commonly live in families led by experienced older individuals; male life histories and associations differ.

**Field question:** Why is 'African elephant' not necessarily a precise modern species identification? Look for an observable feature or action rather than assuming an unseen purpose, personality, sex, health or family relationship from a single image.

## Wild conservation and scientific interpretation

Ivory poaching, habitat fragmentation, conflict with crops and roads; forest and savannah elephants have different IUCN assessments and population trajectories.

## Specific Dubbo zoo evidence and history

Historical Taronga publications and zoo maps document African elephants in the Dubbo collection; an official retrospective says the zoo mourned the passing of Australia's last African elephant. The record is a **former holding**, not evidence the zoo currently has African elephants. Avoid merging this with its present Asian-elephant programme.

**This is not a current 2026 zoo census.** A public map establishes an exhibit label, while a dated Taronga news release supports only the event stated for that date. Animals may be transferred, housed behind the scenes or absent from public view; no names, sexes, photo dates or exact current herd sizes are invented here.

## Zoo-field observation and evidence protocol

Observe from designated public areas, noting the actual date/time, site map grid, number visible and visible feeding/resting/moving/social interactions. Record habitat features, weather and vantage point when known. Do not diagnose health, captivity stress or breeding success from one still photo. Conservation breeding supports genetic management but does not replace protection of wild habitat or prove reintroduced survival.
