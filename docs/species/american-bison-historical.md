## Identification, taxonomy and range

Very large North American bovine with massive front shoulders, shaggy neck, pronounced hump and comparatively slender hindquarters. Unlike buffalo, bison are a distinct bovine group; animals called water buffalo belong to a different lineage.

Taxonomic names, local subspecies, domestic forms and conservation categories must align. Where the old zoo map uses a broad label or conflicting name, keep uncertainty until a dated Taronga animal-holdings record verifies the taxon.

## Diet, behaviour and ecology

Large grazers of the Great Plains and some forest/parkland regions. Seasonal movements, grazing, wallowing and dung redistribute nutrients; herds can profoundly influence prairie biodiversity. Females rear reddish-brown calves; bulls compete during the rut.

## Conservation ecology and threats

The near-destruction of bison populations in the nineteenth century involved commercial killing, colonial displacement and destruction of Indigenous food systems. Recovery in fenced/private herds differs from restoration of free-roaming, genetically diverse, ecologically functioning populations.

**Assessment limit:** Near Threatened often cited (assessment/version to recheck). Wild population figures require a publication year and explicit denominator (mature breeders versus all ages). Zoo populations and reintroduced herds must not be added to wild estimates without a method that does so.
