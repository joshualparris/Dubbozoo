# Asian small-clawed otter — Dubbozoo species research

**Inventory number:** 25/61 · **Scientific identification:** *Aonyx cinereus (formerly Amblonyx cinereus)* · **Conservation:** Vulnerable (IUCN; assessment version check).  
**Research reviewed:** 18 October 2026. This is **not** a Josh-supplied photo observation.

## Identification, taxonomy and range

Smallest commonly described otter, with short partly webbed toes and tiny claws leaving sensitive hands useful for probing mud. Long whiskers assist underwater tactile investigation; do not confuse its hand-like dexterity with primate hands.

Taxonomic names, local subspecies, domestic forms and conservation categories must align. Where the old zoo map uses a broad label or conflicting name, keep uncertainty until a dated Taronga animal-holdings record verifies the taxon.

## Diet, behaviour and ecology

Social wetlands and mangrove otter eating crabs, molluscs and other small aquatic prey. Groups communicate with vocalisations, scent and grooming; breeding adults may be assisted by older family members. Clean wetlands and healthy crustacean resources are essential.

Research prompt: What structures make the paws better at detecting prey than grasping fish with long claws? When recording an observed animal, state the count, surroundings and directly seen behaviour rather than extrapolating kinship or medical condition.

## Conservation ecology and threats

Wetland loss, pollution, overfishing, human conflict and illegal pet trade. Pet-trade demand is incompatible with its specialised social and ecological requirements.

**Assessment limit:** Vulnerable (IUCN; assessment version check). Wild population figures require a publication year and explicit denominator (mature breeders versus all ages). Zoo populations and reintroduced herds must not be added to wild estimates without a method that does so.

## Taronga Western Plains Zoo connection

Taronga documented **three oriental small-clawed otter pups born 8 January 2015** to Emiko and Pocket at Dubbo, and the zoo's 2026 Wild Asia map still lists 'Otters'. 'Oriental' and 'Asian' are alternative common names for this species; do not treat as two entries.

**Evidence policy:** published Taronga reports are dated records, not a live animal database. The exhibit location, keeper-talk time, current animal names, breeding status and count should be checked before a future visit; no personal sighting is asserted in this guide.
