## Physical identification and life history

Tiny grassland finch with bright red/orange bill and black-and-white tail pattern. Adult males often have orange cheek patches, barred chest and chestnut flanks while female markings are subtler.

## Food web, habitat and behavioural ecology

Feeds mostly on grass seeds, often taking insects in breeding contexts, and nests colonially or in small groups. Breeding responds strongly to rainfall and availability of seeds in inland Australia; highly social flocking is an adaptation to variable conditions.

## Risks and conservation actions

Broadly abundant in natural Australian range, but habitat changes, feral predators and changed grasslands can affect local groups. Species splits between Australian and Timor zebra finches mean taxon-specific status must be recorded.
