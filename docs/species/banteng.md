## Identification, taxonomy and range

Wild South-East Asian cattle with conspicuous pale 'stockings' and white rump patch. Bulls are generally darker than females; horns curve and differ with sex. These are not the same as domestic water buffalo.

Taxonomic names, local subspecies, domestic forms and conservation categories must align. Where the old zoo map uses a broad label or conflicting name, keep uncertainty until a dated Taronga animal-holdings record verifies the taxon.

## Diet, behaviour and ecology

Herbivores feeding on grasses, leaves and bamboo with seasonal movements between open grazing habitats and forest shelter. Herds can vary greatly in size; soil disturbance and grazing cycle nutrients.

Research prompt: How can hybridisation with domestic cattle undermine a protected wild cattle population? When recording an observed animal, state the count, surroundings and directly seen behaviour rather than extrapolating kinship or medical condition.

## Conservation ecology and threats

Illegal hunting, forest loss, livestock disease and hybridisation with domestic cattle threaten the genetic integrity of remaining wild populations.

**Assessment limit:** Endangered (IUCN commonly cited; version check). Wild population figures require a publication year and explicit denominator (mature breeders versus all ages). Zoo populations and reintroduced herds must not be added to wild estimates without a method that does so.

## Taronga Western Plains Zoo connection

Taronga's **8 August 2024** Dubbo keeper report states it first acquired banteng in **1981** and had **six** at time of that report. This is a dated snapshot rather than a guaranteed October 2026 herd count.

**Evidence policy:** published Taronga reports are dated records, not a live animal database. The exhibit location, keeper-talk time, current animal names, breeding status and count should be checked before a future visit; no personal sighting is asserted in this guide.
