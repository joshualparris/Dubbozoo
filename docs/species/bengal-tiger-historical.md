# Tiger historically called 'Bengal' — historical Dubbo animal research

**Scientific name / historical classification:** *Panthera tigris — historical 'Bengal' label; lineage unverified*  
**Region:** Asia · **Dubbo evidence:** historical first-party / official record · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.

> This is an archival holding, **not** a verified October 2026 public exhibit and **not** a user-submitted photograph. Avoid projecting named historical individuals or past husbandry into the present.

## Historic Dubbo evidence

A 2003 speech to the NSW Legislative Assembly describes Bengal tiger exhibits at Western Plains Zoo during 1979–84; it is distinct from later Sumatran tiger holdings.

## Identification and scientific distinctions

Tigers are striped solitary felids. The historical 'Bengal' label may reflect signage and trade terminology, not genetic confirmation of Panthera tigris tigris or purity of lineage, especially in older captive collections.

## Natural history and ecological function

Ambush predator of medium-large ungulates, living at low densities across large ranges. Territorial scent marking and vocalisations help avoid direct conflicts; females raise young for a prolonged period.

## Conservation and research limitations

Poaching, prey loss and habitat fragmentation remain threats to wild tigers. Do not count animals from historic mixed zoo lineages in a wild subspecies conservation estimate. Species-level conservation does not identify historical zoo individuals.

Scientific status must be matched to the **taxon, assessment date and population unit**. Historic zoo breeding records demonstrate care, not improved wild populations. The exact species/subspecies or purity of historical animals may be uncertain without acquisition or breeding registers.
