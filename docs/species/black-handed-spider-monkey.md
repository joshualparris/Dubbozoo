# Black-handed spider monkey — Dubbozoo species research

**Inventory number:** 22/61 · **Scientific identification:** *Ateles geoffroyi (Geoffroy's spider monkey)* · **Conservation:** Endangered; taxon/subspecies-specific assessment to recheck.  
**Research reviewed:** 18 October 2026. This is **not** a Josh-supplied photo observation.

## Identification, taxonomy and range

Central American arboreal primate with extremely long limbs and a powerful **prehensile tail** with a sensitive underside near the tip. Unlike ring-tailed lemurs, spider monkeys can suspend themselves by the tail. Colour is variable across subspecies.

Taxonomic names, local subspecies, domestic forms and conservation categories must align. Where the old zoo map uses a broad label or conflicting name, keep uncertainty until a dated Taronga animal-holdings record verifies the taxon.

## Diet, behaviour and ecology

Predominantly fruit-eating, requiring large forest areas with fruiting trees. They travel through canopy, often in smaller temporary groups that split and reunite. Female dispersal and slow reproductive rates affect recovery. As fruit eaters they disperse seeds widely.

Research prompt: How does the tail function as a fifth limb, and why does it change canopy travel? When recording an observed animal, state the count, surroundings and directly seen behaviour rather than extrapolating kinship or medical condition.

## Conservation ecology and threats

Deforestation, hunting, roads and wildlife trade threaten them. Slow births and dependence on mature canopy make isolated forest fragments vulnerable.

**Assessment limit:** Endangered; taxon/subspecies-specific assessment to recheck. Wild population figures require a publication year and explicit denominator (mature breeders versus all ages). Zoo populations and reintroduced herds must not be added to wild estimates without a method that does so.
