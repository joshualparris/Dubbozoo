# Brazilian tapir (historical) — historical Dubbo animal research

**Scientific name / historical classification:** *Tapirus terrestris*  
**Region:** Americas · **Dubbo evidence:** historical first-party / official record · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.

> This is an archival holding, **not** a verified October 2026 public exhibit and **not** a user-submitted photograph. Avoid projecting named historical individuals or past husbandry into the present.

## Historic Dubbo evidence

Taronga's World Tapir Day keeper account describes two Brazilian tapirs at Dubbo, including nine-year-old male L, living with guanaco, greater rhea and Patagonian cavy.

## Identification and scientific distinctions

Large dark South American odd-toed ungulate with a flexible prehensile snout, three rear toes and four forefoot toes. Juveniles display pale stripes and spots which fade with age.

## Natural history and ecological function

Browses fruit, leaves and aquatic vegetation; swims well, uses riparian corridors and disperses large forest seeds. Solitary outside some social/reproductive contexts; important for seed dispersal.

## Conservation and research limitations

Deforestation, hunting, roads and wetland decline affect populations. Exact population numbers and IUCN assessment year should be sourced separately; a captive animal's keeper biography is a dated record, not a modern census.

Scientific status must be matched to the **taxon, assessment date and population unit**. Historic zoo breeding records demonstrate care, not improved wild populations. The exact species/subspecies or purity of historical animals may be uncertain without acquisition or breeding registers.
