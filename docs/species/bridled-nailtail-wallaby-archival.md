# Bridled nailtail wallaby — historical Dubbo zoo research

**Scientific identification:** *Onychogalea fraenata* · **Region:** Australia · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.  
**Evidence grade:** NSW parliamentary answer confirms formal sanctuary holding, 1996. **Not claimed as current 2026 animal or owner-supplied photograph.**

## Scientific identity and distinguishing features
Small, shy Australian wallaby with a distinctive pale 'bridle' from shoulder behind the neck and a stiff, nail-like projection at the end of its tail. The common name is not interchangeable with the northern nailtail wallaby or the extinct crescent nailtail species.

## Ecology, diet, sociality and threats
Feeds on herbs, grasses and small shrubs and shelters in dry grassland/woodland. The bony tail-tip feature helps distinguish the species, while colour/pattern alone is insufficient for a distant photo. It is extremely vulnerable to introduced cats and foxes, pasture changes, small-population genetics and grazing competition.

## Dubbo-specific historical evidence and limitations
NSW Legislative Council Questions and Answers dated 30 October 1996 answers parts (6)–(8) about a transfer from a Queensland captive colony at Pallarenda to Western Plains Zoo. It explicitly says **nine bridled nailtail wallabies were held in the zoo's Australian Native Flora and Fauna Sanctuary** at that time. That nine is a 1996 snapshot, not a 2026 census. The parliamentary exchange also reports death after transport in the particular transfer; it must not be rewritten as proof of nine successful breeders. This is confirmed **behind-the-scenes conservation holding**, not automatically public display.

### Research quality and uncertainty
A dated first-person diary, later scan/photo, zoo map or Taronga press statement support different kinds of facts. A retroactively uploaded film picture does **not** acquire a 2026 original photo date. The animal's existence at the zoo in a visitor account does not prove permanent collection ownership, breeding outcome, current enclosure, genetic lineage, or conservation benefit. Maintain a distinct `evidence_class` in the master inventory, and ask Taronga for accession records before calling the all-time species register complete.
