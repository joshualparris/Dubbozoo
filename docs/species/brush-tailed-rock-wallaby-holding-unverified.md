## Physical identification and life history

Small-to-medium macropod with a black-tipped bushy tail, facial cheek stripe, grey-brown coat and gripping feet for cliff environments. The tail aids balance but is not used to hang from branches.

## Food web, habitat and behavioural ecology

Shelters in rocky crevices and forages for grasses, herbs and leaves, typically venturing out in low-light periods. Small colonies require connected rock habitat to exchange animals and genes.

**Field-research question:** Why can connected rocky refuges determine survival even when grassland remains nearby? Make notes about observable motion, feeding signs and substrate/habitat. Do not infer age, sex, stress or parentage without records.

## Risks and conservation actions

Fox predation, habitat fragmentation, bushfire, drought and loss of genetic diversity. Predator control and secure habitat can matter more than simply breeding wallabies in captivity.
