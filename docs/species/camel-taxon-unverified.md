# Camel (map label; probable dromedary) — Dubbozoo researched profile

## Primary-source taxonomic resolution — Arabian dromedary verified

Taronga's [24 June 2023 keeper article, 'Happy World Camel Day!'](https://www.taronga.org.au/about/newsroom/posts/happy-world-camel-day), written by keeper Ruby Cullen, explicitly identifies resident **Samera** as an **Arabian/Dromedary Camel, *Camelus dromedarius***. Taronga reports she was born in the Northern Territory in **2006** and arrived in Dubbo in **2007**. It describes a one-humped camel, hay and browse feeding, coat shedding and dust-bathing. This is direct keeper provenance for the **species held at Dubbo**, resolving the older generic 'Camel' map-label ambiguity. It does not certify the identities or the number of other historical or current camels. **Scientific species confidence: high for Samera; current 2026 presence not independently checked.**

## Species identity and identification

Dromedaries have one hump; domestic Bactrian camels have two. The 2026 map says only 'Camel', so a rigorous checklist must not silently replace the exhibit label with a conclusive identification without a keeper page, animal photo or accession record.

**Native range / habitat:** Dromedaries originate from human-managed arid regions; feral populations live in Australia; true wild Bactrian camels are a different taxon. Where the exhibit label lacks a confirmed scientific identification, this description is **conditional** on future species verification.

## Feeding, behaviour and natural history

Camel humps store **fat rather than liquid water**. Large feet, eye/nostril protection, flexible body temperature and water conservation aid desert travel. Camels are herbivores and can browse spiny vegetation.

**Field question:** How many humps are actually visible in a dated verified photograph? Look for an observable feature or action rather than assuming an unseen purpose, personality, sex, health or family relationship from a single image.

## Wild conservation and scientific interpretation

The conservation of truly wild Bactrian camels must not be conflated with management of abundant domestic/feral dromedaries; they are different conservation questions.
