# Capybara (historical Dubbo lead) — historical Dubbo zoo research

**Scientific identification:** *Hydrochoerus hydrochaeris* · **Region:** Americas · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.  
**Evidence grade:** May 1995 diary; absent/uncertain by 1999; Sydney capybara records not proof of Dubbo. **Not claimed as current 2026 animal or owner-supplied photograph.**

## Scientific identity and distinguishing features
Largest living rodent, barrel-shaped with partly webbed feet, blunt muzzle and eyes/nostrils placed high on head. Not a pygmy hippo and not the related guinea pig, despite shared rodent lineage.

## Ecology, diet, sociality and threats
Semi-aquatic South American grass eater, living in social groups along wetlands and rivers, entering water to avoid heat and predators. Requires waterside grazing, shelter, and managed aquatic access.

## Dubbo-specific historical evidence and limitations
A firsthand school journal written **May 1995** describes capybaras in Dubbo's mixed South American exhibit with tapirs, guanacos, maras and rheas. The same visitor speculated that capybaras might have gone by their next visit in **1999**; this **does not prove a departure date**. Taronga's modern capybara encounter and its 2021–22 annual-report reference belong to **Taronga Zoo Sydney** and cannot be reused as Dubbo confirmation. Status: visitor-reported historic exhibit, accession still unverified.

### Research quality and uncertainty
A dated first-person diary, later scan/photo, zoo map or Taronga press statement support different kinds of facts. A retroactively uploaded film picture does **not** acquire a 2026 original photo date. The animal's existence at the zoo in a visitor account does not prove permanent collection ownership, breeding outcome, current enclosure, genetic lineage, or conservation benefit. Maintain a distinct `evidence_class` in the master inventory, and ask Taronga for accession records before calling the all-time species register complete.
