# Chital / spotted deer — historical Dubbo zoo research

**Scientific identification:** *Axis axis* · **Region:** Asia · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.  
**Evidence grade:** 1995 diary and firsthand 2010 chital paddock observation. **Not claimed as current 2026 animal or owner-supplied photograph.**

## Scientific identity and distinguishing features
Slender spotted deer of South Asian woodlands with pale spots retained in adulthood; males carry three-tined antlers, with seasonal variation. Unlike fallow deer, its spots commonly remain prominent, though photographs require careful ID.

## Ecology, diet, sociality and threats
Herbivore grazing grasses and selecting leaves/fruit, frequently associating with forest-edge grazing areas. Alarm calls and mixed-herd alertness help detect predators; populations shift seasonally with forage and water.

## Dubbo-specific historical evidence and limitations
The **May 1995 visit journal** names chital deer. A separately dated **28 June 2010** firsthand visitor report notes fallow deer housed together with chital while exhibits were rearranged. The combination is credible **historical public display** evidence. Beware that a different 2014–15 Taronga annual-report entry for a chital deer shelter is specifically **Taronga Sydney**, not Dubbo.

### Research quality and uncertainty
A dated first-person diary, later scan/photo, zoo map or Taronga press statement support different kinds of facts. A retroactively uploaded film picture does **not** acquire a 2026 original photo date. The animal's existence at the zoo in a visitor account does not prove permanent collection ownership, breeding outcome, current enclosure, genetic lineage, or conservation benefit. Maintain a distinct `evidence_class` in the master inventory, and ask Taronga for accession records before calling the all-time species register complete.
