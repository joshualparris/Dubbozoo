# Collared peccary — historical Dubbo zoo research

**Scientific identification:** *Dicotyles tajacu (also Pecari tajacu in older usage)* · **Region:** Americas · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.  
**Evidence grade:** 1995 diary and 1999 zoo visitor photograph; no zoo accession retrieved. **Not claimed as current 2026 animal or owner-supplied photograph.**

## Scientific identity and distinguishing features
Pig-like American artiodactyl with grizzled coat, pale shoulder/neck collar and relatively short legs. Peccaries are in **Tayassuidae**, not the domestic pig family Suidae; common labels and modern genus usage vary.

## Ecology, diet, sociality and threats
Social omnivore of cactus, fruit, roots, seeds and invertebrates in scrub, dry forest and tropical habitats; forages with a highly sensitive snout. Lives in groups that defend ranges by scent communication; births and breeding require records to confirm, not solely a juvenile-looking animal.

## Dubbo-specific historical evidence and limitations
A first-person journal dated **6 May 1995**, reproduced by its writer in June 2026, recalls pigs at the end of Dubbo's circuit and retrospectively identifies them as collared peccaries. The same author uploaded a photograph captioned **Collared Peccary (July 1999)** in the Taronga Western Plains Zoo archive. A specialist commercial historical picture archive independently labels a Dubbo collared peccary. This triangulates a highly plausible former zoo exhibit, but none is an official Taronga acquisition or breeding record. The uploader's 2026 iPhone EXIF date concerns digitisation, **not the 1999 analogue capture date**.

### Research quality and uncertainty
A dated first-person diary, later scan/photo, zoo map or Taronga press statement support different kinds of facts. A retroactively uploaded film picture does **not** acquire a 2026 original photo date. The animal's existence at the zoo in a visitor account does not prove permanent collection ownership, breeding outcome, current enclosure, genetic lineage, or conservation benefit. Maintain a distinct `evidence_class` in the master inventory, and ask Taronga for accession records before calling the all-time species register complete.
