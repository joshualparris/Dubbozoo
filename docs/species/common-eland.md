# Common eland — Dubbozoo researched profile

**Checklist ID:** 9 / 61 · **Taxon:** *Taurotragus oryx* · **Conservation assessment:** Least Concern generally reported; verify Red List version.  
**Researched:** 18 October 2026 · **Evidence class:** published Taronga records and taxon background; **no photo from Josh verified for this entry**.

## Species identity and identification

A particularly large antelope with short, spiralled horns, pendulous throat skin/dewlap and a tawny coat sometimes marked with pale stripes. Both males and females can have horns; do not identify sex simply by the presence of horns.

**Native range / habitat:** Open woodland and savannah across eastern/southern Africa. Where the exhibit label lacks a confirmed scientific identification, this description is **conditional** on future species verification.

## Feeding, behaviour and natural history

Mixed feeder, consuming grasses, leaves and browse. Large herds may form and break apart in response to forage and breeding. Despite body size, eland can jump impressively high from a standing position.

**Field question:** Which anatomical differences separate a giant eland from a horse or cow? Look for an observable feature or action rather than assuming an unseen purpose, personality, sex, health or family relationship from a single image.

## Wild conservation and scientific interpretation

Regional pressure from habitat fragmentation, hunting and access to grazing/water, although the species as a whole is relatively widespread compared with addax or bongo.

**Status caution:** Least Concern generally reported; verify Red List version. Historical counts and today's population should never be substituted for one another.

## Specific Dubbo zoo evidence and history

Taronga's March/April 2026 conservation visitor update reports **six eland calves** and **more than 150 eland calves born at Dubbo since 1976**. The zoo's African Savannah precinct describes a mixed-species habitat. The birth total is historical cumulative information, not current herd size.

**This is not a current 2026 zoo census.** A public map establishes an exhibit label, while a dated Taronga news release supports only the event stated for that date. Animals may be transferred, housed behind the scenes or absent from public view; no names, sexes, photo dates or exact current herd sizes are invented here.

## Zoo-field observation and evidence protocol

Observe from designated public areas, noting the actual date/time, site map grid, number visible and visible feeding/resting/moving/social interactions. Record habitat features, weather and vantage point when known. Do not diagnose health, captivity stress or breeding success from one still photo. Conservation breeding supports genetic management but does not replace protection of wild habitat or prove reintroduced survival.
