# Common wombat / bare-nosed wombat — historical Dubbo research

**Scientific name:** *Vombatus ursinus* · **Historical zoo holdings:** professionally documented, specific opening/closing dates still unknown. **No Josh photo observation**.

## Distinguishing the three species

The common wombat has a comparatively **bare, moist nose**; southern and northern hairy-nosed wombats belong to *Lasiorhinus*. They share powerful digging limbs, rear-facing pouches and hindgut fermentation, but their ranges, facial characters, ecological requirements and threats differ. Species should never be combined into a single 'hairy-nosed wombat' listing.

## Direct Dubbo husbandry evidence

The [Australian zoo-keeper's *Wombat Husbandry Manual*, Donna Treby 2004 revised 2020](https://aszk.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Wombat-Husbandry.pdf), says **Western Plains Zoo held a major captive population of common wombats** and describes a special-purpose underground burrow/reproductive research facility constructed **1997**. [A separate common wombat husbandry manual](https://nswfmpa.org/Husbandry%20Manuals/Published%20Manuals/Mammalia/Common%20Wombat%20%28Elliott%29.pdf) records Catriona MacCallum's Dubbo research adjusting pen layouts and successful breeding, including a report of wombat twins. These are strong species-specific **historical Dubbo facility/animal** links, not just Taronga-wide annual-report headings.

This research should be treated as behind-the-scenes breeding/husbandry unless contemporary visitor records establish a public-facing exhibit. No precise zoo inventory total can be inferred from a statement like 'largest captive population' at one unspecified publication time.

## Ecology, health and conservation

Bare-nosed wombats are Australian burrowers that feed on grasses and other low vegetation, with specialised dentition and slowly digested fibrous diets. They help engineer soil and habitat through burrowing. Threats vary by state/population: roads, dogs, mange (*Sarcoptes scabiei*), drought and habitat disturbance; **Tasmania/common wombat subpopulation conservation status is not automatically the same as mainland status**.

Wildlife-care notes and breeding design are not grounds for suggesting amateur intervention. Animals should be observed from safe distances; rescue or medical care should be handled by licensed specialists.
