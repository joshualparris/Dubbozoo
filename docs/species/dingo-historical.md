# Dingo (historical) — historical Dubbo animal research

**Scientific name / historical classification:** *Canis dingo / Canis familiaris dingo (taxonomic usage contested)*  
**Region:** Australia · **Dubbo evidence:** historical first-party / official record · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.

> This is an archival holding, **not** a verified October 2026 public exhibit and **not** a user-submitted photograph. Avoid projecting named historical individuals or past husbandry into the present.

## Historic Dubbo evidence

A 2003 NSW parliamentary historical statement explicitly lists dingoes among Dubbo zoo exhibits from 1979–84; an old visitor photograph independently supports the historical connection.

## Identification and scientific distinctions

Native Australian canid varying from sandy yellow to darker coats, with upright ears and athletic body form; coat colour by itself cannot verify dingo ancestry, which may require genetic context.

## Natural history and ecological function

Generalist predator/scavenger helping shape prey and competitor populations; packs/family groups and home ranges vary with region and food. Human feeding and unmanaged interaction can create harm.

## Conservation and research limitations

Persecution, livestock conflict, hybridisation debates and disease affect management. Taxonomic and conservation classifications are contested and region-specific. A historic zoo dingo should not be automatically identified as genetically 'pure'.

Scientific status must be matched to the **taxon, assessment date and population unit**. Historic zoo breeding records demonstrate care, not improved wild populations. The exact species/subspecies or purity of historical animals may be uncertain without acquisition or breeding registers.
