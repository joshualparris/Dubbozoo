# Domestic guinea pig — historical Dubbo zoo research

**Scientific identification:** *Cavia porcellus (domestic)* · **Region:** Europe and domesticated · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.  
**Evidence grade:** May 1995 eyewitness children’s zoo stable report. **Not claimed as current 2026 animal or owner-supplied photograph.**

## Scientific identity and distinguishing features
Domestic cavy, much smaller than a capybara, with no external tail and rounded body. Coat and ear forms are diverse domestic varieties rather than distinct wild species.

## Ecology, diet, sociality and threats
Social herbivorous rodent needing fibrous hay/grass, vitamin C in diet, secure retreat space and appropriate husbandry. Wild guinea pig relatives are South American but domestic pet/welfare management is a different issue from wildlife recovery.

## Dubbo-specific historical evidence and limitations
The visitor's contemporary May 1995 school journal describes **guinea pigs inside the Cobb & Co stables in the Children's Zoo**. This is a specific, plausible historical education exhibit location; **no official holdings register was retrieved**. A domestic species, not eligible for a wild-IUCN category as though it were a threatened native mammal.

### Research quality and uncertainty
A dated first-person diary, later scan/photo, zoo map or Taronga press statement support different kinds of facts. A retroactively uploaded film picture does **not** acquire a 2026 original photo date. The animal's existence at the zoo in a visitor account does not prove permanent collection ownership, breeding outcome, current enclosure, genetic lineage, or conservation benefit. Maintain a distinct `evidence_class` in the master inventory, and ask Taronga for accession records before calling the all-time species register complete.
