# Eastern (mountain) bongo — Dubbozoo researched profile

**Checklist ID:** 12 / 61 · **Taxon:** *Tragelaphus eurycerus isaaci* · **Conservation assessment:** Critically Endangered at eastern subspecies conservation assessment; assess rank carefully.  
**Researched:** 18 October 2026 · **Evidence class:** published Taronga records and taxon background; **no photo from Josh verified for this entry**.

## Species identity and identification

A reddish-orange, heavily white-striped forest antelope; both sexes have spiralling horns, unlike many antelope. The lowland/western bongo belongs to a different form of the same species and should not be given eastern bongo wild population figures.

**Native range / habitat:** Highland forests of central Kenya. Where the exhibit label lacks a confirmed scientific identification, this description is **conditional** on future species verification.

## Feeding, behaviour and natural history

Browses leaves, shoots and forest-floor plants in dense vegetation; movements and foraging in steep forest are influenced by cover and human disturbance. Calves initially remain hidden near the group.

**Field question:** Why can a large forest antelope be harder to protect than its impressive appearance suggests? Look for an observable feature or action rather than assuming an unseen purpose, personality, sex, health or family relationship from a single image.

## Wild conservation and scientific interpretation

Forest fragmentation, agricultural encroachment, trapping, hunting and disease risks threaten the tiny native highland populations. Conservation programmes work on habitat and genetic diversity as well as managing captive populations.

**Status caution:** Critically Endangered at eastern subspecies conservation assessment; assess rank carefully. Assessment authority, taxonomic rank, year and whether quoted quantities count all individuals or only mature breeders should be matched before adding headline population figures. Historical counts and today's population should never be substituted for one another.

## Specific Dubbo zoo evidence and history

Taronga identifies an Eastern Bongo **breeding herd in Dubbo**. It documented Djembe giving birth to Jabali on **1 December 2019**, with sire Kulungu; historical named animals cannot be assigned to any current 2026 photo. Taronga reported Maisha's international transfer and quarantine before arrival in 2018.

**This is not a current 2026 zoo census.** A public map establishes an exhibit label, while a dated Taronga news release supports only the event stated for that date. Animals may be transferred, housed behind the scenes or absent from public view; no names, sexes, photo dates or exact current herd sizes are invented here.

## Zoo-field observation and evidence protocol

Observe from designated public areas, noting the actual date/time, site map grid, number visible and visible feeding/resting/moving/social interactions. Record habitat features, weather and vantage point when known. Do not diagnose health, captivity stress or breeding success from one still photo. Conservation breeding supports genetic management but does not replace protection of wild habitat or prove reintroduced survival.
