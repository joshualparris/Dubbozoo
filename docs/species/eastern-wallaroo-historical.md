# Eastern wallaroo (euro/common wallaroo complex) — historical Dubbo zoo research

**Scientific identification:** *Osphranter robustus (formerly Macropus robustus)* · **Region:** Australia · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.  
**Evidence grade:** 1995 first-person exhibit lead; exact wallaroo lineage unresolved. **Not claimed as current 2026 animal or owner-supplied photograph.**

## Scientific identity and distinguishing features
Stocky, powerfully built macropod occupying rocky slopes and dry woodland; coats range from dark to reddish/grey with sex and local variation. 'Eastern wallaroo' can be used for eastern common-wallaroos, while 'euro' and other geographic forms are classified variably.

## Ecology, diet, sociality and threats
Browses/grazes grasses and plants among rocky hills, sheltering in crevices and shade when hot. May form loose feeding groups or be solitary; taxon lineage and geographic provenance need verification.

## Dubbo-specific historical evidence and limitations
The May 1995 firsthand zoo diary lists 'Eastern Wallaroos' in the macropod area. No original sign photograph or Taronga Dubbo collection inventory was recovered. It is **not sound to assume the original 1995 label unambiguously identifies the modern subspecies** of *O. robustus*. Status: first-person visitor evidence only.

### Research quality and uncertainty
A dated first-person diary, later scan/photo, zoo map or Taronga press statement support different kinds of facts. A retroactively uploaded film picture does **not** acquire a 2026 original photo date. The animal's existence at the zoo in a visitor account does not prove permanent collection ownership, breeding outcome, current enclosure, genetic lineage, or conservation benefit.
