# European fallow deer — Dubbozoo research guide

**Number:** 61/61 · **Scientific taxon:** *Dama dama* · **Conservation/context:** Least Concern globally, but invasive where introduced in Australia.  
**Reviewed:** 18 October 2026 · **Photo evidence:** none supplied for this animal; claims are sourced zoo/species research, not observations.

## Scientific identification and adaptations

Medium-sized deer with long tails, distinctive white rump patch and spotted coats in many individuals; coat colours vary from light to very dark. Adult male bucks develop broad palmate antlers, shed and regrow them annually; females generally lack antlers.

## Food, reproduction and ecological roles

Grazes and browses grassland-woodland edges, often in herds. Seasonal rut produces displays and competition among bucks; antler size cannot establish exact individual age or current breeding status.

**Field question:** How do antlers differ from permanent horns, and why do male fallow deer regrow them? Record time, location, number seen and directly observable actions without guessing health, social status or individual name.

## Threats, conservation and scientific limitations

Wild fallow deer in native European regions and introduced feral populations in Australia have very different conservation contexts. In Australia deer can damage native vegetation, compete with livestock and spread pests.

The assessment shown here is a dated conservation/legal context, **not a fresh population estimate**. Legal rankings in NSW, federal Australia and the IUCN may differ; wild, domestic, conservation-breeding and hospital animals must remain separate. For unknown taxonomy, no precise species-level Red List label should be assigned until confirmed.
