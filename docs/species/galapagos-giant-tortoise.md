## Identification, taxonomy and range

A very large terrestrial tortoise with heavy domed or saddle-shaped shell, columnar limbs and a long neck for browsing. Shell shape relates partly to habitat and ancestry, but it cannot definitively identify lineage from an unsourced photo.

Taxonomic names, local subspecies, domestic forms and conservation categories must align. Where the old zoo map uses a broad label or conflicting name, keep uncertainty until a dated Taronga animal-holdings record verifies the taxon.

## Diet, behaviour and ecology

Mainly herbivorous; consumes grasses, leaves, fruits and cacti depending on island and season. Long life spans, delayed maturity and dispersal of seeds make it an important ecosystem engineer, but slow breeding makes populations vulnerable to adult losses.

## Conservation ecology and threats

Historical exploitation, habitat conversion and introduced goats/rats/cats/dogs disrupted many island tortoise lineages. Galápagos taxonomic splits mean older blanket 'Vulnerable' labels can obscure critically threatened species and extinct lineages.

**Assessment limit:** Not one universal IUCN category: island-specific species have differing conservation assessments. Wild population figures require a publication year and explicit denominator (mature breeders versus all ages). Zoo populations and reintroduced herds must not be added to wild estimates without a method that does so.
