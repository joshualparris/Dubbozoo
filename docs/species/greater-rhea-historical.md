# Greater rhea (historical) — historical Dubbo animal research

**Scientific name / historical classification:** *Rhea americana*  
**Region:** Americas · **Dubbo evidence:** historical first-party / official record · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.

> This is an archival holding, **not** a verified October 2026 public exhibit and **not** a user-submitted photograph. Avoid projecting named historical individuals or past husbandry into the present.

## Historic Dubbo evidence

Taronga's historical Brazilian tapir exhibit account explicitly lists Greater Rhea as one of the South American species present at Dubbo.

## Identification and scientific distinctions

Large flightless South American ratite with grey-brown feathering, long legs, three toes and no ostrich-style two-toed foot. The smaller Darwin's/Lesser Rhea is a different taxon.

## Natural history and ecological function

Omnivorous open-grassland forager of leaves, grasses, seeds, insects and other small animals. A male may incubate a communal nest and rear chicks; ecology depends on large, safe grasslands.

## Conservation and research limitations

Agriculture, hunting, dog predation and road barriers fragment grassland populations. Check species-specific status without confusing Greater Rhea with Lesser Rhea.

Scientific status must be matched to the **taxon, assessment date and population unit**. Historic zoo breeding records demonstrate care, not improved wild populations. The exact species/subspecies or purity of historical animals may be uncertain without acquisition or breeding registers.
