# Guanaco (historical) — historical Dubbo animal research

**Scientific name / historical classification:** *Lama guanicoe*  
**Region:** Americas · **Dubbo evidence:** historical first-party / official record · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.

> This is an archival holding, **not** a verified October 2026 public exhibit and **not** a user-submitted photograph. Avoid projecting named historical individuals or past husbandry into the present.

## Historic Dubbo evidence

Taronga lists guanaco among four co-housed South American animals in a Dubbo tapir keeper report, and 2011–12 annual-report movements confirm Guanaco transfers.

## Identification and scientific distinctions

Wild camelid of South America with slender body, long neck, padded feet and soft insulating coat, typically cinnamon/brown above and pale below. It differs from the domesticated llama and alpaca and from the rare wild vicuña.

## Natural history and ecological function

Grazes/browses on grasses and shrubs from Andean foothills to Patagonian steppe. Territorial males may defend female groups; some bachelor bands range widely.

## Conservation and research limitations

Livestock competition, fencing and hunting can affect local herds; wild and domestic camelids have different management regimes. Do not identify an older zoo guanaco's regional ancestry without records.

Scientific status must be matched to the **taxon, assessment date and population unit**. Historic zoo breeding records demonstrate care, not improved wild populations. The exact species/subspecies or purity of historical animals may be uncertain without acquisition or breeding registers.
