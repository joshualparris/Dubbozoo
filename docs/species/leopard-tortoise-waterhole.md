# Leopard tortoise (Waterhole, historic) — historical Dubbo zoo research

**Scientific identification:** *Stigmochelys pardalis (formerly Geochelone pardalis)* · **Region:** Africa · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.  
**Evidence grade:** 2020 Taronga planned arrival; 2022 firsthand zoo species list; official actual arrival date not yet confirmed. **Not claimed as current 2026 animal or owner-supplied photograph.**

## Scientific identity and distinguishing features
Large terrestrial tortoise of eastern and southern African savannah with conspicuous yellow/black patterned high domed shell. Shell pattern/size is variable with growth; not related to Galápagos tortoises at the species level.

## Ecology, diet, sociality and threats
Predominantly grazes dry grasses and other herbaceous vegetation, obtaining moisture through diet and suitable water access. Temperature and humidity regulation, UV exposure and safe winter facilities are crucial in captivity.

## Dubbo-specific historical evidence and limitations
Taronga's 2020 Dubbo keeper article says **four leopard tortoises were expected to move into the temperature-controlled Waterhole meerkat night facility 'later this year'**. That establishes a concrete plan, **not the animals' arrival**. A 2022 firsthand species inventory lists leopard tortoises but the same visitors elsewhere reported an enclosure that could be empty or tortoises indoors; presence is plausible but inconsistent by day. No dated official Taronga record confirming successful arrival has been recovered, so classification remains **planned exhibit with reported subsequent presence**, not first-party accession.

### Research quality and uncertainty
A dated first-person diary, later scan/photo, zoo map or Taronga press statement support different kinds of facts. A retroactively uploaded film picture does **not** acquire a 2026 original photo date. The animal's existence at the zoo in a visitor account does not prove permanent collection ownership, breeding outcome, current enclosure, genetic lineage, or conservation benefit. Maintain a distinct `evidence_class` in the master inventory, and ask Taronga for accession records before calling the all-time species register complete.
