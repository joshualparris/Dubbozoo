# Mala (rufous hare-wallaby) — historical Dubbo zoo research

**Scientific identification:** *Lagorchestes hirsutus* · **Region:** Australia · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.  
**Evidence grade:** Australian Government National Recovery Plan verifies captive programme ended 2001. **Not claimed as current 2026 animal or owner-supplied photograph.**

## Scientific identity and distinguishing features
A small, long-eared hare-wallaby adapted to arid central Australian dunes and spinifex environments. It is a marsupial, not a rabbit; colour and ear shape do not reveal exact subspecies or population origin.

## Ecology, diet, sociality and threats
Feeds on grass seeds, shoots, herbs and plant material, sheltering by day among dense spinifex and low cover. Conservation depends on predator exclusion, suitable fire patterns and gene-flow planning; populations disappeared across much of the mainland after cat/fox impacts.

## Dubbo-specific historical evidence and limitations
The Australian Government's Rufous Hare-wallaby National Recovery Plan explicitly states that **Western Plains Zoo housed mala in its captive programme and released some into larger enclosures in 2001; many died and the zoo discontinued the programme**. This confirms actual animals at Dubbo independently of a visitor's retrospective recollection of a labelled 'Mala' enclosure in 1999. The report does not establish how many animals were originally kept, their precise origin or cause of each death.

### Research quality and uncertainty
A dated first-person diary, later scan/photo, zoo map or Taronga press statement support different kinds of facts. A retroactively uploaded film picture does **not** acquire a 2026 original photo date. The animal's existence at the zoo in a visitor account does not prove permanent collection ownership, breeding outcome, current enclosure, genetic lineage, or conservation benefit. Maintain a distinct `evidence_class` in the master inventory, and ask Taronga for accession records before calling the all-time species register complete.
