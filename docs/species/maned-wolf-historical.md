# Maned wolf (historical) — historical Dubbo animal research

**Scientific name / historical classification:** *Chrysocyon brachyurus*  
**Region:** Americas · **Dubbo evidence:** historical first-party / official record · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.

> This is an archival holding, **not** a verified October 2026 public exhibit and **not** a user-submitted photograph. Avoid projecting named historical individuals or past husbandry into the present.

## Historic Dubbo evidence

Taronga's 2008–09 annual report records a keeper lecture on maned wolf husbandry at Taronga Western Plains Zoo. A later historical hobbyist register suggests multiple breeding/import events, but those details have not been accession-verified.

## Identification and scientific distinctions

Tall reddish South American canid with extraordinarily long black legs, large ears and a dark shoulder mane. It is neither a true wolf of genus Canis nor a fox, but the sole living species of Chrysocyon.

## Natural history and ecological function

Mostly solitary, crepuscular/nocturnal omnivore of small vertebrates, insects and fruit; the wolf apple Solanum lycocarpum is a distinctive component of some Cerrado diets. It uses scent marks and calls across territories.

## Conservation and research limitations

Cerrado conversion, roads, persecution and infections shared with dogs pose threats. The IUCN global assessment of 2015 is Near Threatened and explicitly flagged as needing updating; avoid claiming its 2015 mature-animal estimate is a 2026 count.

Scientific status must be matched to the **taxon, assessment date and population unit**. Historic zoo breeding records demonstrate care, not improved wild populations. The exact species/subspecies or purity of historical animals may be uncertain without acquisition or breeding registers.
