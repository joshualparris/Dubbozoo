# Nilgai (blue bull) — historical Dubbo zoo research

**Scientific identification:** *Boselaphus tragocamelus* · **Region:** Asia · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.  
**Evidence grade:** 1995 diary; historic former-nilgai enclosure recollection. **Not claimed as current 2026 animal or owner-supplied photograph.**

## Scientific identity and distinguishing features
The largest Asian antelope; tall straight limbs, shorter pointed horns on mature males and sexually differing tawny/blue-grey coats. It is a bovid, not a domestic ox, despite 'blue bull'.

## Ecology, diet, sociality and threats
Grazes and browses grasses, legumes, shrubs and agricultural vegetation in Indian open forests, scrub and grassland. Herd structure and movement adapt to seasons; human-wildlife conflict in farming landscapes and grazing competition can be significant locally.

## Dubbo-specific historical evidence and limitations
The retrospective transcription of an original **May 1995** child's detailed zoo visit lists nilgai in a deer/antelope paddock. Later independent visitors identify a **former nilgai enclosure**, subsequently used for plains zebras and then blackbuck, at Dubbo. Together these are a credible historical visitor record, not an independently recovered original Taronga animal accession. **Current presence is not established.**

### Research quality and uncertainty
A dated first-person diary, later scan/photo, zoo map or Taronga press statement support different kinds of facts. A retroactively uploaded film picture does **not** acquire a 2026 original photo date. The animal's existence at the zoo in a visitor account does not prove permanent collection ownership, breeding outcome, current enclosure, genetic lineage, or conservation benefit. Maintain a distinct `evidence_class` in the master inventory, and ask Taronga for accession records before calling the all-time species register complete.
