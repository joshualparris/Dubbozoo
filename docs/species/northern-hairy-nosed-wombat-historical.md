# Northern hairy-nosed wombat — forgotten 1996–97 Dubbo conservation programme

**Scientific name:** *Lasiorhinus krefftii* · **EPBC (2026):** Critically Endangered · **Record category:** verified historical **behind-the-scenes conservation programme**, not a public exhibit or current zoo holding. **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.

## Evidence chronology — strongest first

- The [Queensland Government's parliamentary answer dated 18 December 1996, printed in the 30 January 1997 Hansard](https://documents.parliament.qld.gov.au/events/han/1997/970130ha.pdf) confirms a northern hairy-nosed wombat captive programme at **Western Plains Zoo Dubbo**, initially planning four animals (two males/two females). Its statements about planned numbers are **plans, not verified actual transfers**.
- In a [Queensland ministerial answer dated 19 February 1997](https://documents.parliament.qld.gov.au/TableOffice/questionsAnswers/2003-1995/Answers1997.pdf), the minister reports a **single captive death at Western Plains Zoo** (specifically correcting an allegation of two). The dead wombat was an approximately two-year-old male captured **21 June 1996**. The veterinary report identified intestinal volvulus and intussusception. **There were no other northern hairy-nosed wombats at Dubbo and no further capture permits**, according to this answer.
- A [3 June 1997 answer in the same parliamentary archive](https://documents.parliament.qld.gov.au/TableOffice/questionsAnswers/2003-1995/Answers1997.pdf) states that programme efforts were shifting to husbandry work with **southern hairy-nosed wombats**. Two deaths discussed in that debate include the Dubbo captive death and one trapping-related death in Queensland: not two deaths at Dubbo.
- Both the [Australasian Society of Zoo Keeping wombat husbandry manual](https://aszk.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Wombat-Husbandry.pdf) and [northern hairy-nosed wombat recovery plan](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/biodiversity/threatened/recovery-plans/recovery-plan-northern-hairy-nosed-wombat-lasiorhinus-krefftii-2004-2008) corroborate the historical effort.

### Careful interpretation

There is a firm historical **Dubbo holding** of *L. krefftii*, but no basis to claim the proposed second male/two females ever arrived. The February 1997 answer explicitly says none remained. No user-supplied photos, individual names or actual display dates are known. These events show the enormous difficulties of experimental conservation husbandry and warrant accurate, non-sensational treatment.

## Species identification and ecology

This is a Queensland native **wombat species distinct from southern hairy-nosed wombats** (*L. latifrons*) and common/bare-nosed wombats (*Vombatus ursinus*). A sturdy marsupial excavator with hairy muzzle, powerful claws and large burrow systems, it eats native grasses and shelters by day in deep burrows. In suitable habitat, multiple burrow systems can support movements across grassland and woodland soils. Its ecology is **not interchangeable** with other wombat species when designing recovery programmes.

## Wild conservation 2026

Australia's [DCCEEW priority species profile](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/biodiversity/threatened/action-plan/northern-hairy-nosed-wombat) lists it **Critically Endangered under the EPBC Act**. Key threats include historic clearing and grazing impacts, introduced canids, competition, restricted distribution and low genetic diversity. National profile reports a **2021 estimate of ~315 across two sites**; this is a **historic estimate, not a 2026 count**. Queensland reported 2025–26 breeding at a third reintroduction site (Powrunna), showing recovery work is ongoing. [QLD first wild-born Powrunna joeys](https://www.detsi.qld.gov.au/our-department/news-media/mediareleases/2025/first-wild-born-wombats-sighted); [Queensland current recovery site](https://www.qld.gov.au/environment/plants-animals/conservation/threatened-species/featured-projects/northern-hairy-nosed-wombat).
