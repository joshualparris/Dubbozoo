## Species identity and identification

The largest living bird: flightless, long-necked, with extremely powerful legs and only **two toes** on each foot. Sex-associated plumage patterns occur in adults, but juveniles and lighting can complicate visual identification.

**Native range / habitat:** African open semi-arid savannah and dry country. Where the exhibit label lacks a confirmed scientific identification, this description is **conditional** on future species verification.

## Feeding, behaviour and natural history

Omnivorous leaning plant-based (grasses, seeds, leaves plus invertebrates); swallows grit/stones to aid gizzard grinding. Males and females may share incubation; nest arrangements and social systems vary.

**Field question:** Count the toes and compare an ostrich's legs with a kangaroo's. Look for an observable feature or action rather than assuming an unseen purpose, personality, sex, health or family relationship from a single image.

## Wild conservation and scientific interpretation

Fencing, hunting and disturbance create local pressures; farmed ostrich populations are not straightforward evidence of healthy wild ecosystems.

**Status caution:** Least Concern widely cited; version check required. Assessment authority, taxonomic rank, year and whether quoted quantities count all individuals or only mature breeders should be matched before adding headline population figures. Historical counts and today's population should never be substituted for one another.

Record habitat features, weather and vantage point when known. Do not diagnose health, captivity stress or breeding success from one still photo.
