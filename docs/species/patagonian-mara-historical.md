# Patagonian mara / cavy (historical) — historical Dubbo animal research

**Scientific name / historical classification:** *Dolichotis patagonum*  
**Region:** Americas · **Dubbo evidence:** historical first-party / official record · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.

> This is an archival holding, **not** a verified October 2026 public exhibit and **not** a user-submitted photograph. Avoid projecting named historical individuals or past husbandry into the present.

## Historic Dubbo evidence

Taronga's archived World Tapir Day exhibit profile includes Patagonian Cavy in the same Dubbo South American habitat as Brazilian tapirs.

## Identification and scientific distinctions

Large long-legged South American rodent, not a tiny deer or hare, with elongated ears, powerful hindquarters and an upright sitting posture; related to guinea pigs/capybaras in the cavy family.

## Natural history and ecological function

Feeds on grasses and herbs across arid Argentine shrub-steppe. Usually socially monogamous breeding pairs can join communal nursery groups, with adults returning to feed offspring; habitat use and social patterns vary.

## Conservation and research limitations

Agricultural habitat conversion, hunting, dogs and livestock competition can affect wild populations. Don't conflate historic Patagonian cavy holdings with more recent capybara attractions in Sydney.

Scientific status must be matched to the **taxon, assessment date and population unit**. Historic zoo breeding records demonstrate care, not improved wild populations. The exact species/subspecies or purity of historical animals may be uncertain without acquisition or breeding registers.
