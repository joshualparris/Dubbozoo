# Ring-tailed lemur — Dubbozoo researched profile

**Checklist ID:** 18 / 61 · **Taxon:** *Lemur catta* · **Conservation assessment:** Endangered (IUCN).  
**Researched:** 18 October 2026 · **Evidence class:** published Taronga records and taxon background; **no photo from Josh verified for this entry**.

## Species identity and identification

Recognisable by a long black-and-white ringed tail and pale face with dark eye patches. It is more terrestrial than many other lemurs, but still sleeps, travels and feeds in trees.

**Native range / habitat:** Dry forest and scrubland of southern and southwestern Madagascar. Where the exhibit label lacks a confirmed scientific identification, this description is **conditional** on future species verification.

## Feeding, behaviour and natural history

Social groups are typically female-centred. They eat fruit, leaves, flowers and other seasonal foods, scent-mark with specialised glands and use the conspicuous tail for group visibility and signalling rather than hanging from it like a spider monkey.

**Field question:** Why might a distinctive tail be useful even when a primate does not use it to grip branches? Look for an observable feature or action rather than assuming an unseen purpose, personality, sex, health or family relationship from a single image.

## Wild conservation and scientific interpretation

Habitat clearance, fuelwood collection, hunting and pet trade; severe fragmentation makes safe dispersal hard.

**Status caution:** Endangered (IUCN). Assessment authority, taxonomic rank, year and whether quoted quantities count all individuals or only mature breeders should be matched before adding headline population figures. Historical counts and today's population should never be substituted for one another.

## Specific Dubbo zoo evidence and history

Taronga's historical release describes **two groups arriving from a Hong Kong zoo in 2011** and settling in at Dubbo, including a family and bachelor group. That is a documented introduction, **not** current group composition.

**This is not a current 2026 zoo census.** A public map establishes an exhibit label, while a dated Taronga news release supports only the event stated for that date. Animals may be transferred, housed behind the scenes or absent from public view; no names, sexes, photo dates or exact current herd sizes are invented here.

## Zoo-field observation and evidence protocol

Observe from designated public areas, noting the actual date/time, site map grid, number visible and visible feeding/resting/moving/social interactions. Record habitat features, weather and vantage point when known. Do not diagnose health, captivity stress or breeding success from one still photo. Conservation breeding supports genetic management but does not replace protection of wild habitat or prove reintroduced survival.

## Primary IUCN conservation assessment

The [IUCN 2020 *Lemur catta* Red List assessment](https://doi.org/10.2305/IUCN.UK.2020-2.RLTS.T11496A115565760.en), **assessed 9 May 2018**, lists ring-tailed lemur **Endangered (EN), decreasing**. It describes ongoing severe Madagascan forest fragmentation and capture/hunting pressure, and a suspected **at least 50% decline over three generations** at assessment time. Do not confuse Madagascar wild estimates with captive 2011 Taronga transfers.
