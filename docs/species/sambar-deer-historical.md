# Sambar deer — historical Dubbo zoo research

**Scientific identification:** *Rusa unicolor* · **Region:** Asia · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.  
**Evidence grade:** 1995 visitor journal and independent 1999 labelled photograph. **Not claimed as current 2026 animal or owner-supplied photograph.**

## Scientific identity and distinguishing features
Large Asian forest deer, dark coarse coat with strong neck/shoulder build. Males typically carry three-tined antlers; sex and age should not be inferred from one angle. Introduced wild sambar populations also occur in Australia.

## Ecology, diet, sociality and threats
Browses shrubs, fallen fruit, grasses and leaves at forest edges and wetlands, frequently moving in low light. Wild populations need forest cover and access to water; feral Australian deer management differs from conserving native Asian populations.

## Dubbo-specific historical evidence and limitations
A childhood journal written **6 May 1995** and republished in 2026 recalls sambar deer at Dubbo. A separate ZooChat uploader has posted a labelled **Sambar (1999)** photo in the Dubbo zoo category. This is **corroborating visitor/photo history**, not an authorised keeper inventory or present-day zoo count.

### Research quality and uncertainty
A dated first-person diary, later scan/photo, zoo map or Taronga press statement support different kinds of facts. A retroactively uploaded film picture does **not** acquire a 2026 original photo date. The animal's existence at the zoo in a visitor account does not prove permanent collection ownership, breeding outcome, current enclosure, genetic lineage, or conservation benefit. Maintain a distinct `evidence_class` in the master inventory, and ask Taronga for accession records before calling the all-time species register complete.
