# Scimitar-horned oryx — Dubbozoo researched profile

**Checklist ID:** 19 / 61 · **Taxon:** *Oryx dammah* · **Conservation assessment:** Endangered wild recovery; older 'Extinct in the Wild' listings are historical and inconsistent with successful reintroduction.  
**Researched:** 18 October 2026 · **Evidence class:** published Taronga records and taxon background; **no photo from Josh verified for this entry**.

## Species identity and identification

A pale antelope with long backward-curving horns shaped like scimitar blades, a reddish neck and light body, and adaptations for dry country.

**Native range / habitat:** Historically Sahelian grasslands of northern Africa; managed wild reintroduction in Chad. Where the exhibit label lacks a confirmed scientific identification, this description is **conditional** on future species verification.

## Feeding, behaviour and natural history

Grazing/browser diet varies with rains and seasonal pasture; individuals travel as groups across sparse landscapes. Physiology reduces water loss; calves need cover and maternal protection.

**Field question:** Why should a recovery programme track genuinely breeding wild herds, not just zoo births? Look for an observable feature or action rather than assuming an unseen purpose, personality, sex, health or family relationship from a single image.

## Wild conservation and scientific interpretation

Unregulated hunting and pressure on grazing habitat originally caused disappearance from the wild; internationally coordinated release and protection have rebuilt a reintroduced population. The precise Red List reassessment year must be recorded rather than describing modern wild populations as permanently extinct.

**Status caution:** Endangered wild recovery; older 'Extinct in the Wild' listings are historical and inconsistent with successful reintroduction. Assessment authority, taxonomic rank, year and whether quoted quantities count all individuals or only mature breeders should be matched before adding headline population figures. Historical counts and today's population should never be substituted for one another.

### IUCN status cross-check — important source conflict

The [IUCN's December 2023 Red List update](https://iucn.org/press-release/202312/freshwater-fish-highlight-escalating-climate-impacts-species-iucn-red-list) formally **changed scimitar-horned oryx from Extinct in the Wild (EW) to Endangered (EN)** following reintroductions in Chad. Its 2023 summary cites **at least 140 mature free-ranging individuals** and **331 calves born by 2021**. These are dated measures and not an October 2026 census. [Sahara Conservation's project statement](https://saharaconservation.org/news/milestone-the-scimitar-horned-oryx-downlisted-from-extinct-in-the-wild-to-endangered-by-iucn/) corroborates EN on 11 December 2023.

Taronga's [15 January 2026 article](https://www.taronga.org.au/about/newsroom/posts/new-arrivals-make-a-splash) says the species was 'reclassified as critically endangered', which **conflicts with the IUCN's actual 2023 Endangered announcement**. For IUCN category use **EN (2023)** unless a newer matching Red List assessment explicitly supersedes it; retain Taronga's wording as a documented discrepancy rather than silently repeating it. Zoo births and wild reintroduced births are different population measures.

## Zoo-field observation and evidence protocol

Observe from designated public areas, noting the actual date/time, site map grid, number visible and visible feeding/resting/moving/social interactions. Record habitat features, weather and vantage point when known. Do not diagnose health, captivity stress or breeding success from one still photo. Conservation breeding supports genetic management but does not replace protection of wild habitat or prove reintroduced survival.
