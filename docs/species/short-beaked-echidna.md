# Short-beaked echidna — Dubbozoo research guide

## Scientific identification and adaptations

Spiny monotreme mammal with long tubular snout, claws for digging and powerful muscles. Unlike platypus it searches soil and logs for ants/termites and does not swim to hunt; both lay eggs.

## Food, reproduction and ecological roles

Uses electroreception and tactile/smell cues when probing for ants and termites, with long sticky tongue to obtain invertebrate prey. Produces a single leathery egg placed in a temporary pouch-like fold; young hatch and are nourished by milk, later sheltered in a burrow.

**Field question:** How are echidna feeding and reproduction both unlike a reptile's, despite egg-laying? Record time, location, number seen and directly observable actions without guessing health, social status or individual name.

## Threats, conservation and scientific limitations

Roadkill, dogs, inappropriate garden netting and some habitat conversion; echidnas also cope with variable temperatures using burrows and sheltered refuges. Never relocate one without wildlife rescue advice.
