# Sitatunga — historical Dubbo animal research

**Scientific name / historical classification:** *Tragelaphus spekii*  
**Region:** Africa · **Dubbo evidence:** historical first-party / official record · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.

> This is an archival holding, **not** a verified October 2026 public exhibit and **not** a user-submitted photograph. Avoid projecting named historical individuals or past husbandry into the present.

## Historic Dubbo evidence

A dated Taronga keeper account explicitly states a female sitatunga shared the African Savannah near the giraffe bachelor area.

## Identification and scientific distinctions

A shy spiral-horned antelope with elongated splayed hooves adapted to marshy substrate, and a water-resistant-looking shaggy coat. Male horns spiral; don't infer the identity of the particular historical female from an undated visitor photo.

## Natural history and ecological function

Sitatungas feed on reeds, aquatic plants and grasses in papyrus/reedbeds, swamps and floodplain fringes. Their unusual hooves distribute weight on soft surfaces, helping them cross wet ground and hide within cover.

## Conservation and research limitations

Wetland drainage, fragmentation and unregulated hunting affect some populations; status should be checked against IUCN assessment year. A zoo display is not proof of an established breeding population.

Scientific status must be matched to the **taxon, assessment date and population unit**. Historic zoo breeding records demonstrate care, not improved wild populations. The exact species/subspecies or purity of historical animals may be uncertain without acquisition or breeding registers.
