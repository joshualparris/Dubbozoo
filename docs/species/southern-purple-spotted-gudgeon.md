# Southern purple-spotted gudgeon — Dubbozoo research guide

**Number:** 54/61 · **Scientific taxon:** *Mogurnda adspersa* · **Conservation/context:** Endangered under NSW legislation; separate from IUCN global ranking.  
**Reviewed:** 18 October 2026 · **Photo evidence:** none supplied for this animal; claims are sourced zoo/species research, not observations.

## Scientific identification and adaptations

Small robust freshwater fish with mottled olive/dark pattern and occasional purple-red spotted flanks. Reaches around 15 cm, and can be confused with related gudgeons unless morphology and location are checked.

## Food, reproduction and ecological roles

Occupies slow-flowing creeks, river margins and well-vegetated pools; ambush-feeds on aquatic invertebrates and other small prey. Habitat complexity, submerged plants and clean oxygenated water help shelter young fish and enable reproduction.

**Field question:** Why is a zoo moat only a temporary conservation refuge unless the original river habitat is restored? Record time, location, number seen and directly observable actions without guessing health, social status or individual name.

## Threats, conservation and scientific limitations

Water extraction, dry refuges, altered stream flows, habitat loss and predatory/exotic fish harm isolated Murray-Darling populations. Recovery requires both safe temporary refuges and restoring functional river systems.

The assessment shown here is a dated conservation/legal context, **not a fresh population estimate**. Legal rankings in NSW, federal Australia and the IUCN may differ; wild, domestic, conservation-breeding and hospital animals must remain separate. For unknown taxonomy, no precise species-level Red List label should be assigned until confirmed.

## Specific Dubbo zoo history / verification status

**NSW Fisheries formally confirms rescue in February 2019**, with **38 southern purple-spotted gudgeons brought into Dubbo Zoo** from failing local waterways. Fish were held in the Lion Pride moat with shelter pipes/native aquatic plants; NSW Fisheries' later report says captive populations bred and offspring were returned to the Macquarie catchment in **2024 and 2025**, including 56 and 335 respectively released to Buckinbah Creek. A **moat conservation population is not a visitor aquarium exhibit**.

**Interpretation boundary:** exhibition, zoo-managed conservation breeding, temporary wildlife rehabilitation and incidental animals roaming zoo land are **four different categories**. The historical checklist should not imply a public sighting, photograph, current presence or persistent zoo ownership without dated supporting records.
