## Archival correction: species identification and Dubbo exhibit CONFIRMED

Taronga's [2018 Dubbo Aussie Walkthrough report](https://www.taronga.org.au/about/newsroom/posts/joeys-in-the-aussie-walkthrough) identifies **three Swamp Wallaby joeys** ranging from seven to fifteen months, and its [2013 keeper report](https://taronga.org.au/news/2018-07-11/abundance-spring-joeys-way) documents male **Hunter** entering the breeding group. The scientific name *Wallabia bicolor* aligns with the common name **Swamp Wallaby**. Together with separately documented red-necked wallabies, these were **two distinct species at Dubbo historically**, even though the 2026 map generically says 'Wallaby'. Current occupancy remains unknown.

## Scientific identification and adaptations

Dark, stocky wallaby with orange/buff belly and cheek colouring and a strong tail. The appearance can overlap other wallabies; map's plain 'Wallaby' does not prove this taxon.

## Food, reproduction and ecological roles

Mixed feeder browsing leaves, shrubs and grasses in wooded eastern Australian habitats, often active in low-light conditions. Unusual reproductive biology permits overlap of pregnancies in some individuals; reproduction cannot be inferred from a casual enclosure sighting.

**Field question:** What records would prove the zoo has both species rather than one generic wallaby exhibit? Record time, location, number seen and directly observable actions without guessing health, social status or individual name.

## Threats, conservation and scientific limitations

Road deaths, clearing, dog attacks and habitat fragmentation can affect local populations; not globally rare across its full range.

The assessment shown here is a dated conservation/legal context, **not a fresh population estimate**. Legal rankings in NSW, federal Australia and the IUCN may differ; wild, domestic, conservation-breeding and hospital animals must remain separate. For unknown taxonomy, no precise species-level Red List label should be assigned until confirmed.

## Specific Dubbo zoo history / verification status

The earlier inventory used an unsourced third-party list to name swamp wallaby; Taronga's 2026 map **does not resolve wallaby species**. This guide is reference biology only until the precise taxon at Dubbo is keeper verified; do not count both swamp and red-necked wallabies automatically as proven separate holdings.

**Interpretation boundary:** exhibition, zoo-managed conservation breeding, temporary wildlife rehabilitation and incidental animals roaming zoo land are **four different categories**. The historical checklist should not imply a public sighting, photograph, current presence or persistent zoo ownership without dated supporting records.
