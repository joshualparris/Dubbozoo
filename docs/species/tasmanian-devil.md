# Tasmanian devil — Dubbozoo research guide

**Number:** 56/61 · **Scientific taxon:** *Sarcophilus harrisii* · **Conservation/context:** Endangered under Australian legislation (IUCN category/version check).  
**Reviewed:** 18 October 2026 · **Photo evidence:** none supplied for this animal; claims are sourced zoo/species research, not observations.

## Scientific identification and adaptations

Stocky black carnivorous marsupial with white chest/rump markings, powerful jaws and large head. Loud calls, scent-marking and communal feeding sounds may seem dramatic, but alone are not evidence of aggression toward people.

## Food, reproduction and ecological roles

Scavenges carcasses and takes live prey, acting as a native ecosystem cleaner; mostly nocturnal/crepuscular. Females raise young in a pouch and later den; litters compete for four available teats.

**Field question:** How can a disease-free captive population protect genetic diversity while wild transmission remains a threat? Record time, location, number seen and directly observable actions without guessing health, social status or individual name.

## Threats, conservation and scientific limitations

Devil Facial Tumour Disease (two transmissible cancer lineages), roads and small-population pressures. Disease-free insurance breeding and careful population management may preserve diversity for conservation.

The assessment shown here is a dated conservation/legal context, **not a fresh population estimate**. Legal rankings in NSW, federal Australia and the IUCN may differ; wild, domestic, conservation-breeding and hospital animals must remain separate. For unknown taxonomy, no precise species-level Red List label should be assigned until confirmed.

## Specific Dubbo zoo history / verification status

Taronga reports Tasmanian Devils arrived at Dubbo in **late 2007**, with a managed breeding programme from **2008**. It reported **four joeys born in March 2014** to Moretti and **22 births cumulatively since 2008** as of that news; behind-the-scenes breeding areas and public exhibits are distinct.

**Interpretation boundary:** exhibition, zoo-managed conservation breeding, temporary wildlife rehabilitation and incidental animals roaming zoo land are **four different categories**. The historical checklist should not imply a public sighting, photograph, current presence or persistent zoo ownership without dated supporting records.
