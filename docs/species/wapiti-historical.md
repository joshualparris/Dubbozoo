# Wapiti / elk (historical) — historical Dubbo animal research

**Scientific name / historical classification:** *Cervus canadensis (taxonomic usage sometimes Cervus elaphus canadensis)*  
**Region:** Europe and domesticated · **Dubbo evidence:** historical first-party / official record · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.

> This is an archival holding, **not** a verified October 2026 public exhibit and **not** a user-submitted photograph. Avoid projecting named historical individuals or past husbandry into the present.

## Historic Dubbo evidence

Taronga recorded Wapiti calf Kamali born on 5 December 2009 at Dubbo and further calves during 2013. These are definite historical breeding and public display records.

## Identification and scientific distinctions

Large cervid, usually tan-brown with pale rump, muscular neck and very large branching antlers on males; not a reindeer (both reindeer sexes can grow antlers). Status and nomenclature depend on whether Eurasian red deer and North American wapiti are split.

## Natural history and ecological function

Grazes grasses and browse in forest-edge/meadow settings. Rutting males bugle and defend breeding groups, while calves often remain hidden briefly after birth. Distinguish naturally wild range from captive introductions.

## Conservation and research limitations

Populations vary widely with management. A zoo birth in 2009 confirms a held animal but not a present-day group; translocations and later changes require keeper records.

Scientific status must be matched to the **taxon, assessment date and population unit**. Historic zoo breeding records demonstrate care, not improved wild populations. The exact species/subspecies or purity of historical animals may be uncertain without acquisition or breeding registers.
