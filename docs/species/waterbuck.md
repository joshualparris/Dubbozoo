# Waterbuck — historical Dubbo animal research

**Scientific name / historical classification:** *Kobus ellipsiprymnus*  
**Region:** Africa · **Dubbo evidence:** historical first-party / official record · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.

> This is an archival holding, **not** a verified October 2026 public exhibit and **not** a user-submitted photograph. Avoid projecting named historical individuals or past husbandry into the present.

## Historic Dubbo evidence

Taronga directly describes conservation semen collection from a male waterbuck in Dubbo; the 2015–16 report lists waterbuck in the TWPZ column.

## Identification and scientific distinctions

Large semiaquatic-associated African antelope with long coarse brown fur, ringed horns in males and distinct rump-marking patterns across populations. Common and defassa waterbuck forms vary in rump pattern, so do not label the zoo animal's subspecies without records.

## Natural history and ecological function

A mixed grazer/brower of grassy savannah and floodplain edges. Regular water access, concealment in tall grasses and flexible group structures support survival; females raise calves that may hide initially.

## Conservation and research limitations

Regional hunting and habitat fragmentation create pressure; IUCN species status and subspecies identity require a date-matched check. Captive frozen genetic material supports regional breeding without demonstrating any wild population recovery.

Scientific status must be matched to the **taxon, assessment date and population unit**. Historic zoo breeding records demonstrate care, not improved wild populations. The exact species/subspecies or purity of historical animals may be uncertain without acquisition or breeding registers.
