# Western grey kangaroo (historical display) — historical Dubbo zoo research

**Scientific identification:** *Macropus fuliginosus* · **Region:** Australia · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.  
**Evidence grade:** May 1995 first-person zoo visit; not formal accession. **Not claimed as current 2026 animal or owner-supplied photograph.**

## Scientific identity and distinguishing features
Large grey-brown kangaroo, often with dark facial colouring and heavier muzzle, sometimes called 'stinker'. Distinguish from eastern grey kangaroo, red kangaroo and wallaroo through structure, provenance and verified signs.

## Ecology, diet, sociality and threats
Grazes open grassy woodland and shrubland across southern/western Australia; hopping can make longer-distance movements energy efficient. Joey development and breeding respond to forage and drought.

## Dubbo-specific historical evidence and limitations
The 1995 children's zoo visit diary lists **Western Gray Kangaroos** as observed from public access. A separate long-running macropod discussion confirms that Western Plains historically managed more Australian macropod exhibits than today, but lacks a species-level official census. Historic visitor-observed exhibit, **not confirmed Taronga accession**. Free-ranging native kangaroos near Dubbo must not be mistaken for the exhibit species.

### Research quality and uncertainty
A dated first-person diary, later scan/photo, zoo map or Taronga press statement support different kinds of facts. A retroactively uploaded film picture does **not** acquire a 2026 original photo date. The animal's existence at the zoo in a visitor account does not prove permanent collection ownership, breeding outcome, current enclosure, genetic lineage, or conservation benefit. Maintain a distinct `evidence_class` in the master inventory, and ask Taronga for accession records before calling the all-time species register complete.
