# Western quoll (chuditch) — Dubbozoo research guide

**Number:** 57/61 · **Scientific taxon:** *Dasyurus geoffroii* · **Conservation/context:** Vulnerable under Australian EPBC; exact state and IUCN categories differ.  
**Reviewed:** 18 October 2026 · **Photo evidence:** none supplied for this animal; claims are sourced zoo/species research, not observations.

## Scientific identification and adaptations

Small carnivorous marsupial with chocolate-brown coat and large white spots, pointed face and long tail. Distinguish from eastern quoll and spotted-tailed quoll, which differ in range, markings and size.

## Food, reproduction and ecological roles

Nocturnal predator of large invertebrates, reptiles and small mammals, sheltering in logs, rock cracks and burrows. A solitary-ranging hunter, it needs refuges as well as good prey availability.

**Field question:** Why is survival of *wild-born* descendants more informative than how many captive joeys were produced? Record time, location, number seen and directly observable actions without guessing health, social status or individual name.

## Threats, conservation and scientific limitations

Predation by cats/foxes, loss of hollow logs and rocky refuges, habitat decline. Fenced breeding/recovery helps initially; releases beyond fences require sustained predator management and long-term genetic monitoring.

The assessment shown here is a dated conservation/legal context, **not a fresh population estimate**. Legal rankings in NSW, federal Australia and the IUCN may differ; wild, domestic, conservation-breeding and hospital animals must remain separate. For unknown taxonomy, no precise species-level Red List label should be assigned until confirmed.

## Specific Dubbo zoo history / verification status

Taronga's **February 2026** Wild Deserts report states chuditch **bred in the 110 ha Dubbo sanctuary** survived and reproduced for more than 12 months after reintroduction to a predator-managed zone in Sturt National Park. This is stronger outcome evidence than zoo births alone. They are not necessarily on public display.

**Interpretation boundary:** exhibition, zoo-managed conservation breeding, temporary wildlife rehabilitation and incidental animals roaming zoo land are **four different categories**. The historical checklist should not imply a public sighting, photograph, current presence or persistent zoo ownership without dated supporting records.
