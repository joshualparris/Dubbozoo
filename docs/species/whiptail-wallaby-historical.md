# Whiptail wallaby / pretty-face wallaby — historical Dubbo zoo research

**Scientific identification:** *Notamacropus parryi (formerly Macropus parryi)* · **Region:** Australia · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.  
**Evidence grade:** 1995 firsthand visitor claim only. **Not claimed as current 2026 animal or owner-supplied photograph.**

## Scientific identity and distinguishing features
An eastern Australian macropod with graceful build, striking pale face stripes and notably long thin tail. The descriptive name 'whiptail' is not interchangeable with nailtail or bridled nailtail wallabies.

## Ecology, diet, sociality and threats
Open eucalypt forest edges, wooded grasslands and grassy areas; grazer in small social groups with hiding cover. Habitat loss, roads and predation affect local populations; IUCN listing needs current same-taxon recheck.

## Dubbo-specific historical evidence and limitations
The visitor's original **6 May 1995** school journal explicitly listed 'Whiptail ... Wallabies' in the Australian macropod section. No separate zoo map, photographic ID or Taronga accession has been recovered in this investigation, so this is a **single first-person historic lead**, not established managed collection.

### Research quality and uncertainty
A dated first-person diary, later scan/photo, zoo map or Taronga press statement support different kinds of facts. A retroactively uploaded film picture does **not** acquire a 2026 original photo date. The animal's existence at the zoo in a visitor account does not prove permanent collection ownership, breeding outcome, current enclosure, genetic lineage, or conservation benefit.
