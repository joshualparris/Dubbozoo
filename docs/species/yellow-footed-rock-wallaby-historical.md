# Yellow-footed rock-wallaby — historical Dubbo zoo research

**Scientific identification:** *Petrogale xanthopus* · **Region:** Australia · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026.  
**Evidence grade:** 1995 firsthand exhibit; 2010 exhibit maintenance visitor report. **Not claimed as current 2026 animal or owner-supplied photograph.**

## Scientific identity and distinguishing features
A striking rock-dwelling macropod with yellow/orange lower limbs, long banded tail and complex facial patterns. Not the brush-tailed rock-wallaby (*Petrogale penicillata*) or an interchangeable conservation subpopulation.

## Ecology, diet, sociality and threats
Feeds on grasses and shrubs on rocky ranges, using cliff crevices and caves to evade predators and heat. Highly dependent on connected rocky habitat and controlling feral predators, especially foxes.

## Dubbo-specific historical evidence and limitations
The original 1995 journal lists yellow-footed rock wallabies, and a dated **June 2010** independent zoo visit describes their exhibit under renovation. Taronga also refers to yellow-footed rock wallaby conservation among work associated with its two institutions; that combined statement alone does not establish the Dubbo colony. Status: **strong historical visitor exhibit evidence**, but animal accession and dates remain uncertain.

### Research quality and uncertainty
A dated first-person diary, later scan/photo, zoo map or Taronga press statement support different kinds of facts. A retroactively uploaded film picture does **not** acquire a 2026 original photo date. The animal's existence at the zoo in a visitor account does not prove permanent collection ownership, breeding outcome, current enclosure, genetic lineage, or conservation benefit.
