# Sumatran tiger — Dubbo Zoo deep research guide

**Name used by Taronga:** *Panthera tigris sumatrae* · **IUCN presentation on Taronga page:** Critically Endangered (CR) for the Sumatran form · **Reviewed:** 18 October 2026. No submitted tiger photo.

## What is a Sumatran tiger?
Sumatran tigers are the remaining wild tiger lineage on the Indonesian island of Sumatra. Their stripes help break up their outline within forests and vegetation; striped pelage is not a fingerprint without clear reference images. They are powerful, mostly solitary carnivores that need enough forest, wild prey and space to establish territories. Mothers raise cubs for an extended period; a photograph of two tigers near each other does **not** establish a mother–cub link.

A **taxonomy caution:** Taronga uses the traditional subspecies name *P. t. sumatrae*. Some modern tiger taxonomies group Sunda-island tigers differently. An IUCN Red List entry for *Panthera tigris* as a whole is **not automatically the same assessment** as Sumatran population status; maintain source name and rank.

## Wild conservation
Forests are fragmented by roads, development and agriculture; prey is reduced by hunting; and tigers face poaching and conflict with people and livestock. Taronga's [species account](https://www.taronga.org.au/wildlife-and-conservation/animals/detail/sumatran-tiger) describes the Sumatran form as **Critically Endangered**, with approximately **400** wild animals reported in its educational material. This estimate is approximate, not an October 2026 verified census, and must not be confused with the global tiger count across all subspecies/lineages.

Conservation tools include preventing habitat conversion, enforcing protection, preserving prey and supporting informed land-use/palm oil choices. 'Certified sustainable' palm oil needs independent assessment; simply switching to another land-demanding oil is not proof that rainforest land use is improved.

## Taronga Western Plains Zoo
Taronga specifically places Sumatran tigers in its **Wild Asia habitat** and lists a **12:25 pm keeper talk on Monday, Wednesday and Friday** on its species page. A keeper programme listed online may change; verify on the date of the visit. Taronga describes collaborative conservation breeding and genetic management; breeding in care is not equivalent to restoring the animal's habitat in Sumatra.

An older Taronga article describes the challenge of conservation-conscious palm oil shopping, but no specific product endorsement is part of this guide.
