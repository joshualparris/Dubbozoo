Research entry 004 · Taronga Western Plains Zoo

The electric river detective.

Ornithorhynchus anatinus · platypus. It lays eggs, senses the electric signals of underwater prey, and nurses its young without nipples. Dubbo's Platypus Rescue HQ is both a conservation research facility and a public exhibit — not an ordinary zoo enclosure.

Science in Dubbo

Platypus Rescue HQ

Opened in February 2024, the purpose-built $12.1 million refuge supports emergency care, habitat research and rehabilitation. Taronga's public-facing ambassador is reported as Mackenzie, an adult male. Viewing an animal is not guaranteed.

Official visitor map and hours →

Evidence, not myth

What is its conservation status?

Near Threatened on the IUCN Red List, with different legal categories in Victoria, South Australia, NSW and nationally. Australia's federal 2026 Vulnerable nomination was not prioritised for assessment, while ecological studies forecast serious risks under future drought.

Species profile

Small population. Remarkable teamwork.

African wild dogs are an African native canid species, recognised by oversized ears, long legs and individual patches of russet, black and white. Unlike feral domestic dogs, these are an entirely distinct wild species.

IUCN statusEndangered2025 assessment
Mature breeders1,676Estimated globally; not total dog count
World population< 700 packsEstimated, declining
Zoo map19BCurrent Taronga African Adventure guide

Population numbers require care: 1,676 is the IUCN's estimate of mature individuals, not all African wild dogs alive. Older sources' “6,600 adults” is a different measure and should not be compared directly. Read the population methodology.

Why such big ears?

They can rotate to help hear faint sounds and communicate with pack mates. Taronga also highlights their distinctive four-toed front paws.

Life as a pack

Group hunting and cooperative care are central to their lives. Adults and older siblings can help raise young and share food.

Built to travel

Long legs and stamina support movement over huge ranges. Taronga reports speeds as high as 70 km/h.

A patchwork identity

Each coat is different. Researchers and keepers can use patterns to help recognise individuals when linked to confirmed records.

Conservation

Protecting space for the pack.

Habitat fragmentation, conflict with people, snares, roads and disease leave dispersed packs vulnerable. IUCN finds the global population continuing to decline.

Conservation in Zimbabwe

Painted Dog Conservation's teams conduct anti-poaching patrols, rehabilitation, pack monitoring, education and community-led projects around Hwange and other landscapes. Visit the organisation.

Decades of animal care in Dubbo

Taronga says the zoo has housed the species since 1982 and documented past breeding. In 2024 the zoo announced a $1.25m habitat redevelopment. Completion of each stage has not been independently verified. Read Taronga's announcement.
