## What the frames show

### Frames 1–3 — shaded resting sequence
A mane-bearing lion lies on short grass in deep shade with the forelegs extended. The head orientation changes slightly across the three frames. Open habitat, fallen timber, a reddish track and a tree line are visible beyond the animal.

### Frames 4–6 — elevated-log sequence
A mane-bearing lion stands on a large elevated fallen trunk/log structure. The body is side-on and the face is generally oriented toward the camera. The mouth is visibly open in some frames.

## Evidence boundaries

A developed mane strongly suggests a male lion, but sex is not keeper-verified. The two sequences could show the same animal under different lighting/posture or more than one individual. Do not identify the photographed lion as Lwazi, Bahati, Jabari or any other named animal without a keeper-verified image match.

The open mouth does not establish roaring, panting, scent processing, aggression, distress or any other specific cause. The resting posture does not establish sleep duration, heat stress, illness or welfare state. The elevated-log posture does not establish why the lion climbed the structure or how often it uses it.
